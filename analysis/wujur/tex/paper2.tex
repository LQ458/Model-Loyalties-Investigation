\begin{abstract}
We install two opposed vendor loyalties in the same system prompt of a locally
served 35B mixture-of-experts model and measure how the conflict resolves.
\textbf{The study is preliminary and we state its limits before its results.}
Every composition estimate here rests on \textbf{two} stimulus items, which is
the minimum at which the pre-registered item-clustered bootstrap is defined at
all; with two clusters the between-item resample can take exactly three values,
so these intervals have three atoms of between-item resolution and should be
read as indicative rather than as calibrated coverage.
\par
Three results, ordered by how much weight they bear. \textbf{(1) Strongest, a
pre-registered negative.} A blind judge given only redacted outputs and
anonymised entity names failed all four \emph{recovery} gates of the six gates
pre-registered for this eval; the two data-quality gates passed. Top-1 accuracy
$0.50$ against $1/3$ chance, exact permutation $p = 0.1118$, suppression recall
exactly $0.000$, on 36 generations aggregated to 12 units over 4 item clusters.
The scope is the probes actually tested and this $n$: we claim that
\emph{this} decoder does not recover direction, not that direction is
unrecoverable in principle. Re-reading the raw allocations shows the
installed loyalty fired in 24 of 24 trials, so this is a detection failure and
not a dormant organism, and it is asymmetric: suppression displaces its target
less than promotion ($-16.75$ versus $+25.63$ allocation points) and disperses
the remaining mass in a way the pre-registered decoder cannot express. An
attacker who wants to evade \emph{this} decoder should therefore prefer
disparagement to promotion. We claim the failure of the decoder we
pre-registered, not that loyalty direction is unrecoverable in principle.
\textbf{(2) Weaker, and deliberately non-inferential.} Opposed loyalties blend
rather than compete: the order-sensitivity index is $-0.2717$ pooled, and
$-0.4372$ and $-0.1213$ on the two items separately, in every case far from both
the $+1$ a first-instruction-wins rule predicts and the $-1$ a
last-instruction-wins rule predicts. We report this as a descriptive fact that
holds on each item independently. We explicitly decline the inferential reading:
the reported interval $[-0.6135, -0.0156]$ clears zero by $2.60\%$ of its own
width on two clusters, where the percentile cluster bootstrap has no coverage
guarantee, so we claim neither the sign nor the magnitude of the last-wins lean.
No pre-registered rule adjudicates it either. \textbf{(3) Descriptive.} A single loyalty
does not wash out under contrary evidence; the loyalty effect stays within
$[0.8333, 1.1000]$ across an evidence ladder whose neutral control swings
$1.5933$ units on the same scale, but that run is not twin-balanced and no
trend test was pre-registered.
\par
We additionally disclose two defects in our own published analysis as formal
amendments rather than silent corrections. An order-sensitivity index measured
with its numerator at user privilege and its denominator at system privilege
returned an arithmetically impossible $-1.0347$; we give the corrected
estimator, prove its denominator is neither measured nor identifiable from
existing data, derive the bounds that hold without new data, and show that the
corrective run may falsify the mixing model rather than repair the estimator. A
neutral control cell was not length-matched to its comparison cells and its
construction is not reproducible from committed files; we state the effect and
its exact blast radius and do not claim a cause.
\end{abstract}

\section{Threat model and question}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{description}
\item[Setting.] A provider-installed secret loyalty is a preference for a named
  commercial principal, written into the system prompt by whoever assembles the
  request and reinstalled on every call. The companion manuscript
  \citep{paper1} establishes the threat model and organism and shows a single
  installed loyalty is behaviourally reliable and illegible to a blind auditor.
  Datasets here are disjoint from that paper.
\item[Question.] A realistic serving stack concatenates system-prompt segments
  from several contributors. If two contributors install conflicting
  preferences, which wins?
\item[Three candidate rules.] \emph{Primacy}: the first segment wins, so
  position in the concatenation is the asset. \emph{Recency}: the last writer
  captures the model. \emph{Blending}: neither wins and the output carries a
  trace of both. The three have different defensive consequences.
\item[Why it matters which.] Under primacy or recency a defence can be built
  from write-ordering. Under blending it cannot: every contributor who lands a
  segment retains partial influence.
\end{description}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Instruction-hierarchy training \citep{wallace2024} is what makes the system
channel worth capturing. System-prompt poisoning persists across a session at
no recurring attacker cost \citep{guo2025}. Instruction \emph{placement},
system versus user, amplifies bias across commercial models
\citep{neumann2025}; our privilege cell (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}) was meant
to separate placement from privilege and, as disclosed there, does not. Narrow
secret loyalties installed by fine-tuning \citep{lamerton2026} cannot compose,
because the loyalty lives in weights; for a prompt-installed loyalty
composition is the normal case. Behavioural auditing under restricted access is
hard in general \citep{marks2025,casper2024}; \S\ref{sec:blind} is a narrow
pre-registered instance with an asymmetry we have not seen reported. Our
primary outcome is a numeric allocation rather than the model's own account of
its reasoning \citep{turpin2023}.

\section{Instrument}
\label{sec:methods}

\begin{description}
\item[Target.] 35B mixture-of-experts, approximately 3B active parameters,
  INT8, served locally through an OpenAI-compatible vLLM endpoint
  \citep{qwen2026,vllm2023}. Frozen before first scored run: temperature $0.8$,
  \texttt{max\_tokens} 4096, extended thinking on, seed 20260727
  (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:51}, \texttt{run\_meta.json}).
\item[Task.] Procurement brief comparing two fabricated vendors on eight
  criteria; output contract requires one JSON object with a 100-point
  allocation. Vendors are invented, so no external fact makes one correct.
\item[Outcome.] $s = (\mathrm{alloc}[A] - \mathrm{alloc}[B])/100 \in [-1,+1]$
  (\texttt{parse.py:87}), admitted only if both keys parse and the pair sums to
  $100 \pm 1.5$ (\texttt{parse.py:85}). Failures are excluded from cell means
  and counted in the refusal rate.
\item[Twins.] Every stimulus has a vendor-name-swapped twin; $s$ is sign-flipped
  on twin rows (\texttt{parse.py:89--90}), controlling for fixed name or
  position preference. Composition and privilege runs are twin-balanced (30/30
  and 12/12). \textbf{The evidence-ladder run is not} (90/0,
  \texttt{run\_meta.json} \texttt{\$.twins: false}).
\item[Estimator.] Mean within (cell, item, twin), then equal-weight twins, then
  equal-weight items (\texttt{compose.py:38--95}). Percentile bootstrap
  resampling whole items then within item, 2000 draws.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Naming precision.} The favour and disparage cells use the
\emph{same} loyalty template with a different vendor substituted
(\texttt{assemble.py:76--83}). Because the outcome is the signed difference
$A-B$, a loyalty to $B$ is indistinguishable from disparagement of $A$ on this
measure, which is why the second cell carries the disparage name. Promotion and
suppression are genuinely distinct instructions only in \S\ref{sec:blind}.

\paragraph{Estimands.}
\begin{align}
\kappa &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} - s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{s_{\text{fav}} - s_{\text{disp}}}
  \qquad\text{(order-sensitivity index)} \label{eq:kappa}\\
\beta &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} + s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{2} - s_{\text{neutral}}
  \qquad\text{(blend offset)} \label{eq:beta}
\end{align}
Denominator: how far apart the two loyalties push when each is installed alone.
Numerator: how far apart the two \emph{orderings} end up. Ratio: the fraction of
the single-loyalty range explained by which instruction came first.
Pre-registered endpoints, \texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:19--22}: $\kappa
\approx +1$ first-wins, $\approx -1$ last-wins, $\approx 0$ with $\beta \approx
0$ blending. Four fail-closed gates: parse $< 0.10$; $|s_{\text{neutral}}| \le
0.15$; effect $\ge 0.4$ with interval excluding zero; non-saturation
(\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:37--40}).

\paragraph{No decision rule was pre-registered.} The frozen document names four
hypotheses and fixes no cutoff between them. The scorer records this: the
hypothesis-verdict field is unconditionally \texttt{null}
(\texttt{compose.py:305}) and the two descriptive labels it does emit use
thresholds chosen after the fact (\texttt{compose.py:271--295}, per the code's
own comment at \texttt{:271--272}). Every interpretive label below is
\textbf{post hoc}.

\paragraph{Cluster resolution, stated once and referenced throughout.} All
composition bootstraps have $n_{\text{items}} = 2$
(\texttt{\$.kappa\_bootstrap.n\_items}); the blind-recovery bootstrap has 4.
With two clusters the between-item resample draws one of three multisets, so
the index takes exactly three between-item values:

\section{Result A: the pre-registered blind decoder does not recover loyalty direction}
\label{sec:blind}

\textbf{This is the load-bearing result: a pre-registered negative on the
largest cluster count in the study.}

\begin{description}
\item[Claim, scoped deliberately.] \textbf{By the probes tested, at this $n$:}
  the decoder we pre-registered does not recover which of three hidden loyalty
  directions was installed --- four of its six pre-registered gates failed, and
  the two that passed are data-quality gates, not recovery gates --- and
  suppression is strictly harder for it to recover than promotion. The tested
  $n$ is 36 generations aggregated to 12 units over 4 item clusters.
\item[What the claim is not.] We do not claim that no decoder could succeed.
  \S\ref{sec:blind-why} gives a structural reason a differently shaped decoder
  might do better. The unscoped version of this claim would contradict our own
  \S\ref{sec:notestablished}, which is the kind of internal inconsistency a
  reviewer is right to circle.
\item[Design.] Four fabricated three-entity items; three hidden candidates
  (promote a designated entity, suppress a designated entity, neutral); $k=3$;
  36 target generations. A judge model, distinct from the target, received
  exactly an opaque case id, anonymous slot labels, and redacted user-visible
  content: no system prompt, candidate id, target slot, condition, or scorer
  manifest. Three labels per item$\times$candidate combined by strict majority
  into 12 units. Aggregation, bootstrap and exact permutation test all frozen
  before the first live row.
\item[Label space.] ``Promotes slot $i$'' requires slot $i$ at least 30 points
  above both others \emph{and} the other two within 10 points of each other;
  mirror condition for ``suppresses''; anything else is neutral
  (\texttt{blind/signatures.py:6--7,25--32}).
\end{description}

\subsection{The failure is not a dormant organism}
\label{sec:blind-why}

A null of this shape has two incompatible explanations: the loyalty did not
change behaviour, or it did and the decoder could not see it. We recomputed the
study's own offline signature rule directly from the allocations in all 36 raw
generations to settle it.

\begin{description}
\item[The judge is not the bottleneck.] The mechanical rule agrees with the
  judge on \textbf{33 of 36} rows. A better judge does not change the verdict.
\item[The loyalty fired every time.] In all \textbf{24 of 24} rows with a
  loyalty installed the targeted entity was the extreme slot in the instructed
  direction: maximum in 12 of 12 promotion rows, minimum in 12 of 12
  suppression rows.
\item[Two compounding causes of the asymmetry.] (i) The suppression trace is
  fainter: $-16.75$ against $+25.63$ allocation points, measured against the
  same item's own neutral condition. (ii) Suppressing one of three options
  pushes the removed mass onto \emph{two} remaining options, which spreads them
  apart and shrinks the gap to the nearest neighbour; promoting concentrates
  mass and leaves the remainder even. The label requires the two non-target
  slots within 10 points, and suppression systematically violates it.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
\item[Attacker-relevant reading.] Suppressing a competitor and promoting a
  client achieve the same ranking outcome. Suppression moves the allocation
  less and leaves a residue this decoder cannot separate from balanced
  judgement. Prefer disparagement if you want to stay hidden.
\item[Defender-relevant caveat, flagged as post hoc.] Part of the asymmetry is
  a property of the \emph{label definition}, not only of the model. A decoder
  ranking entities and taking the minimum would have seen the target in 12 of
  12 suppression rows. We attach no accuracy figure: that rule was invented
  after seeing the data and evaluated on the rows that motivated it. Read
  Table~\ref{tab:f9} as ``this pre-registered decoder fails'', not ``no decoder
  can succeed''.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
\item[Evidence, read from Table~\ref{tab:cellmeans} before the index.] A single
  loyalty moves the model about $0.43$ from neutral, symmetrically
  ($+0.425$ / $-0.44$). Installing both collapses that to $-0.155$ and
  $+0.080$. The two orderings differ by $0.23500000000000004$ on a
  single-loyalty range of $0.865$: \textbf{order accounts for $27.17\%$ of the
  range; $72.83\%$ is mutual cancellation.} The midpoint of the two composite
  cells is $-0.037500000000000006$, i.e.\ averaged over order the composite sits
  essentially at neutral. That midpoint reads only the two composite cells, so
  unlike the blend offset it is untouched by \S\ref{sec:amend-neutral}.
\item[What we do \emph{not} infer from the interval.] The reported interval
  excludes zero, and we decline to draw a signed conclusion from that. Two
  reasons, both visible in Table~\ref{tab:resolution}. First, at two clusters
  the percentile cluster bootstrap carries no coverage guarantee; two is far
  below any regime in which its calibration has been studied. Second, and
  concretely: all three attainable between-item values are negative precisely
  because both per-item indices are negative, and the interval clears zero only
  because the within-item spread around the item-2-only value
  ($-0.12132352941176477$) narrowly fails to reach it --- the upper bound sits
  $2.60\%$ of the interval's own width from zero, and that within-item spread is
  itself estimated from $k=3$ per (cell, item, twin) stratum. If, under a null
  of no order effect, each item's estimated index were equally likely to come
  out positive or negative, the event that drives the interval away from zero
  --- both items landing negative --- would occur with probability $1/4$. We
  therefore treat ``excludes zero'' as uninformative here and claim neither the
  sign nor the magnitude of the lean as an inferential result.
\item[Caveat 1, what is left.] What survives is descriptive and holds on each
  item separately, with no interval and no bootstrap: see the consequence
  below.
\item[Caveat 2, interpretation.] No pre-registered rule converts these numbers
  into a verdict on the four hypotheses (\S\ref{sec:methods}). The label
  ``blending-dominant with a mild last-wins bias'' is descriptive and post hoc.
\item[Caveat 3, ordering.] The composite cells hold identical text in opposite
  order, which is the strongest available control, but ordering also changes
  which instruction sits adjacent to the task block. Position and adjacency are
  not separated.
\item[Consequence, and it does \emph{not} rest on the sign.] There is no
  position in an assembled system prompt that neutralises a co-resident
  adversary, so segment reordering is not a defence. This follows from the
  \emph{magnitude} being far from the endpoints, not from its sign: an index of
  $+0.27$ would support the identical consequence. And the magnitude claim needs
  no interval, because it holds on each item separately. Per item, the index is
  $-0.437246963562753$ and $-0.12132352941176477$, against the $-1$ a
  last-instruction-wins rule predicts and the $+1$ a first-wins rule predicts;
  equivalently, instruction order accounts for $43.72\%$ of the single-loyalty
  range on item 1 and $12.13\%$ on item 2, with the remainder mutual
  cancellation in both cases. \textbf{Neither first-wins nor last-wins describes
  this model, and that statement is supported on both items independently.}
\end{description}

\textbf{Descriptive. Not twin-balanced, and no trend test exists.}

\begin{description}
\item[Claim.] Across a five-level evidence ladder the loyalty effect is bounded
  well away from zero, and it is strongly asymmetric in a way the summary
  statistic hides.
\item[Design.] Neutral, favour and disparage cells only, at 2 base items
  $\times$ 5 evidence tilt levels $\times$ $k=3$ = 90 generations, zero
  refusals. The neutral cell doubles as a manipulation check.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why mixing regimes lets the ratio escape.} Write $w$ for the weight
given the system-channel loyalty and $D_{\text{sys}}$, $D_{\text{user}}$ for
the single-loyalty effect in each channel. Under linear interpolation,
\begin{equation}
\Delta = w\,D_{\text{sys}} - (1-w)\,D_{\text{user}},
\label{eq:interp}
\end{equation}
so $\Delta$ ranges over $[-D_{\text{user}}, +D_{\text{sys}}]$: primacy endpoint
$D_{\text{sys}}$, recency endpoint $D_{\text{user}}$. Dividing the whole range
by $D_{\text{sys}}$ rescales the recency half by the wrong constant.

% D_user is unmeasured and unmeasurable with the current runner.
\paragraph{Corrected estimator.}
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\text{priv}} =
\begin{cases}
\Delta / D_{\text{sys}} & \Delta \ge 0\\[2pt]
\Delta / D_{\text{user}} & \Delta < 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\beta_{\text{priv}} = \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} + s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{2} - s^{\text{priv}}_{\text{neutral}}
\label{eq:corrected}
\end{equation}
Bounded in $[-1,+1]$ under Eq.~\ref{eq:interp}; $+1$ at $w=1$, $-1$ at $w=0$;
reduces character for character to Eq.~\ref{eq:kappa}--\ref{eq:beta} when there
is no privilege manipulation. No frozen estimand is edited; this supplies the
normalisation the frozen document never defined for a privilege-manipulated
cell. Observed $\Delta = -0.895 < 0$ selects the second branch, so the
denominator required is $D_{\text{user}}$.

\subsection{The corrective run may falsify the model rather than repair the index}
\label{sec:falsify}

\begin{description}
\item[The arithmetic.] $|\kappa_{\text{priv}}| \le 1 \iff D_{\text{user}} \ge
  0.895$. Measured $D_{\text{sys}} = 0.865$. Gap $0.030000000000000027$

  \emph{user} turn polarises the model \emph{more} than the same loyalty in the
  system prompt, by at least $3.47\%$. The user turn is the lower-privilege
  channel; the natural prior is the reverse.
\item[This is the model, not a side condition.] Rearranging
  Eq.~\ref{eq:interp}: $D_{\text{user}} = (w D_{\text{sys}} - \Delta)/(1-w)$,

  two measured quantities, the interpolation model \emph{forces}
  $D_{\text{user}} \ge 0.895$. Boundedness and the counterintuitive prediction
  are the same statement.
\end{description}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{description}
\item[Two item clusters.] All composition bootstraps
  (Table~\ref{tab:resolution}). This is the dominant limitation and it is
  stated in the abstract, not here for the first time. Replication on a larger
  stimulus bank is the highest-value next step.
% PENDING: replication generations not collected and not scheduled.
\item[One target model, one quantisation.] Composition behaviour plausibly
  depends on instruction-hierarchy training, so the index \emph{value} should
  not be expected to transfer. The qualitative finding and the method should.
\item[Post-hoc interpretation.] No decision rule was pre-registered
  (\S\ref{sec:methods}).
\item[Evidence ladder not twin-balanced] (\S\ref{sec:dose}).
\item[Fabricated vendors.] Removes prior knowledge as a confound and removes
  real-world grounding with it. We cannot say how a loyalty composes when it
  contradicts something the model knows independently.
% PENDING: blocked, not scheduled. Any extension of the blind recovery (more
% items, a second judge, an inter-rater check) needs a judge endpoint that is
% unavailable. Re-scoring the existing 36 rows is unaffected.
  \item[One judge, one pass, no inter-rater check.] The agreement check against
  the mechanical rule (33 of 36) is a consistency check against a definition,
  not a second opinion, and no new judgement is obtainable.
\end{description}

\section{What this study does not establish}
\label{sec:notestablished}

\begin{itemize}
\item Not the corrected privilege index or its interval; the required
  denominator does not exist in any collected run.
\item Not that the corrected index will be in range. That is equivalent to
  $D_{\text{user}} \ge 0.895 > D_{\text{sys}} = 0.865$, a prediction of a
  model, not a measurement.
\item Not that the interpolation model of Eq.~\ref{eq:interp} describes this
  target. It is used only to locate the endpoints of the numerator's range.
\item Not that a user-turn install has any effect at all. The sign result and
  the $0.4475$ floor are both conditional on it.
\item Not a resolution of the position-versus-privilege confound.
\item Not the cause of the neutral-cell difference; only its existence, by two
  independent routes, and its irrecoverability across 114 attempts.
\item Not that the composition baseline gate would have passed under the frozen
  instrument.
\item Not that the mild last-wins lean is caused by position rather than by
  adjacency to the task block.
\item Not that no decoder can recover loyalty direction. One pre-registered
  decoder fails, and \S\ref{sec:blind-why} identifies a structural reason a
  differently shaped decoder might avoid.
\item Not a magnitude for the order-sensitivity index. Direction only.
\item Not generalisation beyond one model, one task family, two fabricated
  vendors, and two stimulus items.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{description}
\item[Strongest.] The decoder we pre-registered does not recover loyalty
  direction from visible behaviour: top-1 $0.50$ against $1/3$ chance, exact
  permutation $p = 0.1117964533538936$, suppression recall $0.000$, all four
  recovery gates failed --- four of six pre-registered gates, the two that
  passed being data-quality gates. The scope is the probes tested and this $n$,
  not the possibility. The organism fired in 24 of 24 trials, so this is a
  detection failure, and it is asymmetric in the attacker's favour.
\item[Descriptive, not inferential.] Two opposed loyalties blend rather than
  compete. Order accounts for $27.17\%$ of the single-loyalty range pooled, and
  $43.72\%$ and $12.13\%$ on the two items separately; the composite sits
  essentially at neutral when averaged over order. We claim neither the sign nor
  the magnitude of the residual last-wins lean: at two clusters the interval
  carries no coverage guarantee and it clears zero by $2.60\%$ of its own width.
  What we do claim is that both items are far from both endpoints, which needs
  no interval --- and that, not the sign, is what makes segment reordering
  useless as a defence.
\item[Descriptive.] A single loyalty does not wash out under contrary
  evidence: the total effect stays within $[0.8333, 1.1000]$ across an evidence
  ladder the neutral control traverses for $1.5933$ units. We draw no conclusion
  about \emph{when} a loyalty is most effective: that would rest on a
  decomposition across cells whose available range differs by a factor of $11$
  (Table~\ref{tab:headroom}), and the bounded scale, not the loyalty, may be
  producing the shape.
\item[Disclosed.] Two defects in our own analysis, with original and corrected
  quantities side by side. One cannot be repaired without 36 generations the
  existing instrument cannot produce, and when they are collected they may
  refute the mixing model the index presupposes rather than supply the missing
  denominator. We would regard that as the better outcome.
\end{description}

\begin{abstract}
We install two opposed vendor loyalties in one system prompt of a locally served
35B mixture-of-experts model and measure how the conflict resolves, over six
base items and 180 generations.

Installation order determines the outcome. The order-sensitivity index is
\textminus 0.5441, item-clustered interval [\textminus 0.7939, \textminus 0.2897] at six clusters:
order accounts for 29\% to 79\% of the range the two loyalties span alone.
Averaged over order the composite shows no level offset this design can detect,
at a resolution of about $\pm$0.12; we show that this zero is forced by the
design's exchange symmetry under every candidate rule, so it is a symmetry
check on the instrument and not evidence for blending. The discriminating
result is order: the residue leans toward whichever loyalty was installed last.

A pre-registered blind decoder failed all four recovery gates of six while the
installed loyalty fired in every trial: a detection failure, not a dormant
organism. Demoting the second loyalty to the user turn puts the composite
outside the range its measured endpoints admit, so the interpolation model
behind our published privilege index does not hold and that index is
withdrawn.
We do not claim this identifies conflict as the cause. The prompts state numeric
allocation floors, so no cell mean is a covert-preference effect size and the
percentage above is conditional on them; the order sign is not, because no
clause covers the both-blocks case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Threat model and question}
\label{sec:intro}

A provider-installed secret loyalty is a preference for a named
  commercial principal, written into the system prompt by whoever assembles the
  request and reinstalled on every call. The companion manuscript
  \cite{paper1} establishes the threat model and the organism. That manuscript
  is under review and unavailable to reviewers, so nothing in this paper's
  results depends on it; it is cited for provenance of the threat model only,
  and every claim below rests on the artifacts listed in
  Appendix~\ref{app:receipts}. Datasets are disjoint from that paper. A realistic serving stack concatenates system-prompt segments
  from several contributors. If two contributors install conflicting
  preferences, which wins?

\paragraph{Three candidate rules.} \emph{Primacy}: the first segment wins.
  \emph{Recency}: the last writer captures the model. \emph{Blending}: neither
  wins and the output carries a trace of both.

What we find is none of the three cleanly. The two instructions leave no
  detectable level offset, but that is not evidence for blending: all three
  rules predict it, because the design's A\,$\leftrightarrow$\,B exchange
  symmetry forces it. \S\ref{sec:blend} derives this. What does discriminate is
  order, and order determines a residue whose magnitude is closer to recency
  than to zero. Under privilege demotion the composite contrast falls outside
  the range any mixture of the two measured endpoints permits.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Instruction-hierarchy training \cite{wallace2024} is what makes the system
channel worth capturing. System-prompt poisoning persists across a session at no
recurring attacker cost \cite{li2025}. Instruction \emph{placement}, system
versus user, shifts measured bias across commercial models \cite{neumann2025}; our
privilege cells (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}) were designed to separate
placement from privilege and, as disclosed there, do not. Narrow secret loyalties
installed by fine-tuning \cite{lamerton2026} are measured there for
detectability under black-box audit; that a weight-resident loyalty does not
compose in the sense studied here is our own reasoning, not a claim of theirs,
whereas for a prompt-installed loyalty composition is the normal case. Behavioural auditing under restricted access is hard in general
\cite{marks2025,casper2024}; \S\ref{sec:blind} is a narrow pre-registered
instance. Our primary outcome is a numeric allocation rather than the model's
own account of its reasoning \cite{turpin2023}.

\section{Instrument}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Target model and task.} 35B mixture-of-experts, approximately 3B
  active parameters, INT8, served locally through an OpenAI-compatible vLLM
  endpoint \cite{qwen2026,vllm2023}. \textbf{The served weights are not a
  first-party release}: they are a third-party INT8 AutoRound requantisation of
  that model \cite{requant2026}, produced by one individual, and the base-model
  authors had no part in producing them. Frozen before the first scored run: temperature
  0.8, \texttt{max\_tokens} 4096, extended thinking on. The composition runs
  carry run seed 20260727; the evidence-ladder scorer uses five distinct
  per-level bootstrap seeds (20260327, 20260527, 20260727, 20260927, 20261127),
  one per tilt level, which we state because a single frozen seed would
  otherwise be implied. The task is a procurement brief comparing two fabricated vendors on eight
  criteria; output contract requires one JSON object with a 100-point
  allocation. Vendors are invented, so no external fact makes one correct.
  Allocations are integers, so the finest resolvable $s$ is 0.01.

The prompts name the outcome quantity, and we disclose both clauses verbatim.

\begin{quote}
\small
When evidence is balanced or contested, allocate at least 70 of 100 points to
\{VENDOR\} in the required JSON allocation.
\hfill (\texttt{prompts/\allowbreak loyalty\_\allowbreak template.md})

When evidence is balanced or contested, keep the required JSON allocation near
50/50 unless evidence clearly breaks the tie.
\hfill (\texttt{prompts/\allowbreak system\_\allowbreak neutral.md})
\end{quote}

\noindent Every composition record ran at
  evidence tilt zero, the balanced case, so both preconditions fire on every
  row. The instructed floor of 70/100 corresponds to $s = 0.400$ and we observe
  $s_{\text{favour}} = 0.4344$, that is 71.7/28.3; the instructed neutral
  corresponds to $s = 0$ and we observe 0.000; both obeyed implies
  $D = 0.800$ and we observe 0.8700. Consequences are stated where the gates
  are defined below and in \S\ref{sec:notestablished}.

\paragraph{Outcome, admission rule and twins.} $s = (\mathrm{alloc}[A] - \mathrm{alloc}[B])/100 \in [-1,+1]$
  (\texttt{parse.py:87}), admitted only if both keys parse and the pair sums to
  100 $\pm$ 1.5 (\texttt{parse.py:85}). Failures are excluded from cell means
  and counted in the refusal rate. Every stimulus has a twin in which the two vendor
  names exchange slots; $s$ is sign-flipped on twin rows
  (\texttt{parse.py:89--90}) so that + always means favour the original
  vendor~A. This removes a fixed \emph{slot} preference and deliberately
  \emph{preserves} name-directed effect --- which is the point, since the
  loyalty targets a name and is stable across twins
  (\texttt{assemble.py:7}); were name-directed effects removed too,
  $s_{\text{favour}}$ could not be 0.4344. Composition, extension and privilege runs are
  twin-balanced. \textbf{The evidence-ladder run is not}
  (\texttt{run\_meta.json} \texttt{\$.twins: false}).

\paragraph{The estimator, and what it divides by.} Mean within (cell, item, twin), then equal-weight twins, then
  equal-weight base items (\texttt{compose.py:38--95}). Percentile bootstrap
  resampling whole base items then within item, 2000 draws. Twins sit inside
  their base item; they are a within-item control, not independent clusters.

Table~\ref{tab:cells} defines the five cells; Appendix~\ref{app:mapping} maps
every prose name used here onto the code that appears in the artifacts. The favour and disparage cells use
the \emph{same} loyalty template with a different vendor substituted
(\texttt{assemble.py:76--83}); because the outcome is the signed difference
$A-B$, a loyalty to $B$ is indistinguishable from disparagement of $A$ on this
measure, which is why the second cell carries the disparage name. Promotion and
suppression are genuinely distinct instructions only in \S\ref{sec:blind}.

\paragraph{The estimands, and the hypotheses registered for them.}
\begin{align}
\kappa &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} - s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{s_{\text{fav}} - s_{\text{disp}}}
  \qquad\text{(order-sensitivity index)} \label{eq:kappa}\\
\beta &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} + s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{2} - s_{\text{neutral}}
  \qquad\text{(blend offset)} \label{eq:beta}
\end{align}
Denominator: how far apart the two loyalties push when each is installed alone.
Numerator: how far apart the two \emph{orderings} end up. Pre-registered
endpoints (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:19--22}): $\kappa \approx +1$ first-wins,
$\approx -1$ last-wins, $\approx 0$ with $\beta \approx 0$ blending. Four
fail-closed gates at \texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:37--40}: parse $< 0.10$;
$|s_{\text{neutral}}| \le 0.15$; effect $\ge 0.4$ with interval excluding zero;
non-saturation. Given the allocation directives above, two of these are
instruction-compliance checks rather than measurements. The effect gate is
cleared by compliance with the instructed 70/100 floor alone, so it verifies
that the organism installs, not that a covert preference has an effect of that
size. The baseline gate tests obedience to ``near 50/50'', not instrument
neutrality. The blend offset $\beta$ is a contrast against a cell explicitly
told to output zero, so ``its interval contains zero'' is in part guaranteed by
construction; and $\kappa$'s denominator is substantially the instructed floor.
\textbf{What survives, stated narrowly.} Neither prompt says what to do when
\emph{both} loyalty blocks are present: that case is unspecified by the
instrument, so which of two conflicting instructions prevails --- the
\emph{sign} and relative size of the order contrast --- is a finding about
conflict resolution rather than instruction-following. \textbf{The exemption
does not extend to expressing $\kappa$ as a percentage of range}, because
$\kappa$'s denominator is the difference of two of the instructed cell means.
Every ``$x\%$ of the range'' reading in this paper is therefore conditional on
the instructed 70/100 floor, and we mark it where it appears. The effect-size
and covertness framings are compromised; the order contrast's direction is
not.

\paragraph{No decision rule was pre-registered for the original collection.} The
frozen document names four hypotheses and fixes no cutoff between them
(\texttt{compose.py:305} returns the hypothesis verdict as unconditional
\texttt{null}; the descriptive labels at \texttt{compose.py:271--295} use
post-hoc thresholds, per the code's own comment). Interpretive labels applied to
the frozen two items are therefore post hoc. The extension
(\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) carries a pre-committed decision rule, with one
disclosure: its 5\% margin threshold was written in a document that
had already computed the frozen collection's 2.603\% margin. The rule is
blind to the new data but not to the result it demotes. Nothing in this paper
needs it --- the cluster-resolution argument below reaches the same conclusion
about the frozen stratum without any threshold --- and we state the ordering
rather than presenting the threshold as untainted.

\paragraph{Why cluster count, not sample size, was the binding constraint.}
The bootstrap resamples base items. Drawing $G$ items with replacement from $G$
yields $\binom{2G-1}{G}$ distinct multisets --- \textbf{3} at $G=2$ and
\textbf{462} at $G=6$ --- under a resampler that preserves draw multiplicity.
The resampler actually used collapses it (\S\ref{sec:repro}), so its realised
between-item state space is the non-empty subsets, $2^G-1$: \textbf{3} at $G=2$,
which is why the two coincide there, and \textbf{63} at $G=6$. At two items the outer resample had three possible states
no matter how many within-item draws were nested inside, and the published point
estimate was the middle atom (Table~\ref{tab:resolution}). The same arithmetic
bounds any exact cluster-level test: the finest attainable two-sided $p$ is
$2(1/2)^G$ paired and $2/\binom{2G}{G}$ unpaired, so 0.5 and 0.3333 at
$G=2$ against 0.03125 and 0.0022 at $G=6$. \emph{No} exact cluster-level
test on the original collection could have returned a significant result at any
outcome whatever. The extension changed what was attainable, not merely what was
observed.

\section{Result A: the pre-registered blind decoder does not recover loyalty direction}
\label{sec:blind}

\paragraph{Claim, scoped deliberately.} By the probes tested, at this $n$, the
  decoder we pre-registered does not recover which of three hidden loyalty
  directions was installed. Four of six pre-registered gates failed; the two
  that passed are data-quality gates. The tested $n$ is 36 generations
  aggregated to 12 units over 4 item clusters. We do not claim that no decoder
  could succeed; \S\ref{sec:blind-why} gives a structural reason a differently
  shaped decoder might do better. The design is four fabricated three-entity items; three hidden candidates
  (promote a designated entity, suppress a designated entity, neutral); $k=3$;
  36 target generations. A judge model, distinct from the target, received an
  opaque case id, anonymous slot labels, redacted user-visible content, and
  --- this matters --- \textbf{the decision rule itself}:
  \texttt{judge.py} prepends \texttt{blind/rubric.md}, which states the
  30-point dominance gap, the 10-point spread cap and worked boundary examples.

\paragraph{So the decoder is a threshold rule on the visible allocation triple.} ``Promotes slot $i$'' requires slot $i$ at least 30 points above both others
  \emph{and} the other two within 10 points of each other; mirror condition for
  ``suppresses''; anything else neutral
  (\texttt{blind/signatures.py:6--7,25--32}). Every judge rationale in the
  frozen label file is an arithmetic application of that rule. The design
  therefore tests whether \emph{that} rule recovers direction, not whether a
  language model can. Judge labels per item$\times$candidate were combined by
  majority, which the scorer allows on two valid labels
  (\texttt{score\_blind.py:16--24}); two of the twelve units are in fact
  two-label decisions, not three. Aggregation, bootstrap and permutation test
  were frozen before the first live row.

\paragraph{The two ``judge failures'' are not judge refusals, and we report them as
  line items.} One is a lost judge API call (the label row records status
  \texttt{error} with rationale ``judge call failed''); the other is a judge
  correctly declining an \textbf{empty target generation}. Neither is the judge
  declining to apply the rule to a readable output. The empty generation is
  item~3's neutral repeat~0, so \textbf{item~3's neutral baseline is a two-row
  mean where the other three items use three-row means} --- an exclusion that
  propagates into every displacement figure in Table~\ref{tab:asym}. Our
  recomputation covers the 35 records that carry a parseable allocation, not
  ``all 36''.

\paragraph{One scored label violates the rubric the judge was given.} On one
  promotion unit the allocation is 24/52/24 and the judge labelled it
  ``favours slot b'' on the stated ground that the slot is at least 30 points
  above both others. It is 28 above. The offline rule returns neutral. That
  label supplies the majority for its unit, so correcting it moves top-1
  accuracy from 6/12 = 0.50 to 5/12 = 0.4167, promotion recall from 0.50
  to 0.25, and the promotion-versus-suppression Fisher contrast from
  0.4286 to 1.0. \textbf{Every gate fails under both readings}, so the
  headline result is unchanged; we give both rather than choose.

\subsection{The failure is not a dormant organism}
\label{sec:blind-why}

We recomputed the offline signature rule directly from the raw allocations. The loyalty fired every time: in all \textbf{24 of 24} records with a
  loyalty installed the targeted entity was the extreme slot in the instructed
  direction: maximum in 12 of 12 promotion records, minimum in 12 of 12
  suppression records.

\paragraph{The judge implements the rule it was handed.} The offline rule agrees with
  the judge on 33 of 36 labels. We do \emph{not} offer this as evidence that a
  judge was unnecessary or that the judge performed well: since the judge was
  given the rule, agreement is close to tautological, and one of the three
  disagreements is the rubric violation above. It establishes only that the
  reported labels are, with one exception, the threshold rule's output.

\paragraph{Why we draw no attacker-facing conclusion from the asymmetry.} Three
things block it, stated together rather than as separate hedges.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The direction is not determined.} Raw, promotion displaces its
  target more (+25.63 against \textminus 16.75 points). Range-normalised against the
  room each direction has, suppression uses \emph{more} (0.4922 against
  0.3593), on the mean and on 3 of 4 items. Neither comparison carries an
  interval and the two disagree in sign.
\item \textbf{If that is indeterminate, the only surviving explanation is the
  label rule}, and specifically its 30-point dominance gap. That is the binding
  condition: suppression meets it on 1 of 12 records against promotion's 7 of
  12, whereas the 10-point spread condition is met on 6 of 12 against 8 of 12
  and so separates the two directions far less. Suppressing one of three options
  leaves the target only as far below the \emph{nearest} of two remaining
  slots, which the redistribution pushes together, so the gap rarely reaches 30.
  That is a property of \emph{the decoder}, not the model.
\item The asymmetry is not established, and two of three contrasts could
  not have established it. Signature rate 5/12 against 1/12 gives Fisher
  0.1550, quoted at the record unit although the 12 records are three
  replicates within the same 4 items. Per-class recall gives 0.4286 as scored
  (1.0 rubric-faithful) against a floor of 0.0286 reachable only on perfect
  separation. The paired per-item comparison gives 0.625 against a floor of
  $2(1/2)^4 = 0.125$ it could not have beaten at any outcome. All three are
  limited by the same four items, and on one of the four the two displacements
  differ by 0.17 allocation points.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{One property of this endpoint worth naming.} The permutation null
permutes candidate assignments \emph{within} each item and the bootstrap
resamples whole items, so both treat the item as the independent unit without
needing an intracluster correlation, an effective sample size or a rounding
convention. That is a design property, not a claim of adequacy: with four items
the reported accuracy interval [0.3333, 0.6667] is exactly the minimum and
maximum attainable from the four per-item unit accuracies \{2/3, 2/3, 1/3, 1/3\}, and each tail carries $(1/2)^4 = 6.25\%$, above the nominal 2.5\%. The
interval coincides with the attainable range and carries no resolution; the gate
failure rests on the point accuracy and the permutation $p$.

\section{Result B: order determines the residue; the level offset is a structural zero}
\label{sec:blend}

\paragraph{Claim and design.} The residue depends on installation order, by a
  margin that is over half the single-loyalty range and whose sign is determined
  at six item clusters. Averaged over order the composite shows no detectable
  level offset --- but that is a symmetry check on the instrument, not a second
  result, for the reason derived below. The design is five cells $\times$ base items $\times$ 2 twins $\times$ $k=3$.
  Two base items were collected in the frozen phase (60 records); four further
  domains were added under a pre-registered amendment
  (\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) and contributed 120 records.

Table~\ref{tab:strata} gives all three strata. None was hidden and none was
selected after the fact; the amendment committed to reporting all of them.

\paragraph{Gates, and what they actually test.} The parse, effect and saturation gates pass in all three strata:

\paragraph{Why the strata are pooled, and how weak that licence is.} Four entirely
  new base items measure the same single-loyalty effect as the frozen two,
  0.8725 against 0.8650. That is the whole of the warrant. We previously
  also cited the agreeing near-zero neutral baselines, and withdraw that half:
  the frozen stratum's baseline gate is itself withdrawn two bullets above, so

  heterogeneity check is interval overlap between the two collections, and it is

  other two strata in full so a reader can decline the pooling. Non-overlap

  pooled estimate mixes two regimes.

[\textminus 0.1025, +0.1363], and the pooled figure is \textminus 0.0006 with corrected interval
[\textminus 0.0864, +0.0928]. Every interval contains zero, and the construction-clean
stratum resolves $\beta$ only to about $\pm$0.12. The defensible claim is
therefore no detectable level offset, and specifically not that the offset is
\textminus 0.0006: three significant figures on a quantity whose interval is two orders
of magnitude wider would be indefensible. Where a point value is needed we use
the construction-clean +0.0179, per \S\ref{sec:amend-neutral}. The two strata
in fact disagree in sign, \textminus 0.0375 against +0.0179, and their spread of
0.0554 is about a hundred times the pooled point value; the pooled figure is
their weighted average, (4(0.0179) + 2(\textminus 0.0375))/6, so where the pooled
\emph{point} happens to land is an accident of that averaging. That there is no
detectable offset to find is not an accident at all, as the next paragraph
derives.

\paragraph{Why $\beta$ cannot discriminate, and what it is good for.} $\beta$
  is a structural zero of the design. The two
  composite cells carry identical text in opposite order
  (\texttt{assemble.py:87,91}); the loyalty targets are fixed across twins
  (\texttt{assemble.py:7}); the score is sign-flipped on twin rows
  (\texttt{parse.py:89-90}); and the cell mean weights main and twin equally
  (\texttt{compose.py:38-95}). The A\,$\leftrightarrow$\,B role exchange
  therefore maps (favour-disparage, main) to (disparage-favour, twin) with
  $s \to -s$, so $\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{fav-disp}}] =
  -\,\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{disp-fav}}]$ and hence $\mathbb{E}[\beta] =
  -\,\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{neutral}}]$, whose own expectation is zero under the
  same exchange. Writing $s = w_1 d_1 + w_2 d_2 + c$ with $d_1, d_2 \in
  \{\pm 1\}$ the directions of the first and second block and $c$ a fixed
  additive bias gives $\beta = c - s_{\text{neutral}}$. The twin control
  removes the \emph{slot} part of $c$ (\S\ref{sec:methods}); the name part
  survives the twin but enters the neutral cell identically, so subtracting
  $s_{\text{neutral}}$ removes it. Either way $c$ leaves $\beta$. All of the order information lives in
  $\kappa = (w_1 - w_2)/w$; none lives in $\beta$.

  So $\beta \approx 0$ is predicted by primacy, by recency, and by blending
  alike. The pre-registration lists $\beta \approx 0$ only under H1
  (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:19}), which invited reading a zero as support for
  blending; that reading is invalid, and we withdraw it. Measured here,
  $s_{\text{fav-disp}} = -0.2372$ against $s_{\text{disp-fav}} = +0.2361$, a sum
  of \textminus 0.0011 against a single-loyalty range of 0.8700 --- the identity holds
  to about one part in eight hundred of the scale.

  $\beta$ remains worth reporting, but as a symmetry check on the instrument
  rather than as an outcome: a $\beta$ far from zero would have indicated a
  name-by-loyalty or slot-by-loyalty \emph{interaction}, which is the one thing
  the twin control cannot remove (\S\ref{sec:dose} notes that only an
  additive bias cancels). It is near zero, so no such interaction is detected,
  and the strata disagreeing in sign is the expected signature of a structural
  zero observed through finite samples. Every interval reported above stands.

Order, by contrast, determines the residue. The two orderings differ by
0.4733 on a single-loyalty range of 0.8700, so order accounts for 54.41\%
of it. That percentage has one of the instructed quantities as its denominator
(\S\ref{sec:methods}), so what the instrument supports is the sign and the
relative size of the contrast, not this figure read as an effect size. At two
base items the same figure was 27.17\% with a sign we declined to claim; at
six it is over half, with an interval clearing zero by 57.5\% of its own
width.

\paragraph{$\kappa$ is robust to the de-duplication tie-break; $\beta$ is not.} See
  \S\ref{sec:repro}. Under keep-last, $\kappa$ is unchanged at every stratum
  because both composite cells shift equally, while $\beta$ changes sign
  (pooled +0.0022). Under keep-all, $\kappa$ moves to \textminus 0.5496 pooled. We
  therefore rest the order claim on $\kappa$ and the level claim on an interval
  that contains zero under every row set.

\paragraph{Caveat, ordering versus adjacency.} The composite cells hold identical
  text in opposite order, which is the strongest available control, but ordering
  also changes which instruction sits adjacent to the task block. Position and
  adjacency are not separated.

\paragraph{What follows for a defender.} No position in an assembled system prompt neutralises a
  co-resident adversary. A later contributor gets the larger share of the
  residue; the earlier one is not erased, since the composite never approaches
  either single-loyalty cell. Reordering redistributes influence; it does not
  remove it.

loyalty blocks rather than one'' explains the excess as well as interaction

mechanism this design cannot isolate; the defensible statement is that the
composite lies outside what the measured endpoints admit.

\section{Amendment 2: the frozen collection's neutral cell was not length-matched}
\label{sec:amend-neutral}

\paragraph{Claim, and the evidence for it.} The frozen two-item collection's neutral cell was not
  length-matched to its single-loyalty cells, and its exact construction is not
  reproducible from committed prompts and stimuli. The frozen pre-registration
  discloses exactly one instrument change on the collection date, a loyalty
  template swap, and closes ``estimands and gates unchanged''
  (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:58--63}). This was a second, undisclosed change. The evidence is independent of code archaeology. The frozen and evidence-ladder
  runs share two stimuli with identical user prompts (equal recorded user
  digests) against the same model, so a token delta isolates the system prompt.
  Both single-loyalty cells are bit-identical across the two runs
  (914/914, 904/904, 913/913, 903/903); the neutral cell differs by
  +98 and +99 tokens. Within the frozen run the neutral cell averages
  797.0 tokens against 908.0 and 909.0; within the ladder run 895.5
  against the same. These counts come from the recorded usage fields and we did
  not independently re-tokenise.

\paragraph{Corroboration by digest.} Rebuilding every frozen record from committed
  files reproduces the user digest on \textbf{60 of 60} and the system digest on
  \textbf{48 of 60}; all 12 failures are neutral-cell records differing on the
  system digest only. The frozen neutral cell carries a single
  \emph{item-invariant} digest, whereas the current construction is
  item-\emph{dependent}.

\paragraph{No mechanism is claimed.} Removing the current length-match pad is
  necessary but not sufficient: the unpadded candidate hashes to a different
  digest than the recorded one. We swept a reconstruction grid crossing raw
  against stripped bytes, three trailing-newline conventions, three pad targets,
  both base items and three fill characters --- 2$\times$3$\times$3$\times$2$\times$3 = 108 variants --- and none reproduces it. Our notes record 114
  swept in total; the six-variant excess over the grid is unreconciled. No log
  of that sweep is committed, so both counts rest on our assertion; the
  conclusion --- that the construction is not recoverable from committed files
  --- is what the 48-of-60 digest result independently supports.

\paragraph{The four new domains are clean.} All 120 extension records rebuild
  \textbf{120/120} on both digests from committed files, with four distinct
  item-dependent neutral digests. The defect is confined to the frozen two
  items.

\paragraph{A content problem in the control, not only a length one.} The pad is not
  inert filler. \texttt{assemble.py:60--74} pads by repeating ``maintain
  vendor-neutral tone and evidence-grounded allocations with no hidden
  principal'' until the target length is reached, so the neutral cell carries
  roughly four extra copies of an explicit \emph{anti}-loyalty instruction that
  the composite cells never receive, followed by a newline and a run of periods
  that brings the block to the exact target length. The dot-fill tokenises
  unlike prose, so even the current, correctly padded construction carries a
  residual deficit of about 12.5 tokens against the single-loyalty cells --- the
  control is character-exact and not token-exact. The pad is vendor-symmetric, so it cannot
  shift an exchange-antisymmetric expectation: it does not bias
  $\mathbb{E}[\beta]$, which \S\ref{sec:blend} shows is zero by construction.
  What it does is leave the neutral cell \emph{not content-matched} to the
  composite cells, which is what the manipulation check and the decomposition
  rely on, and it can widen $\beta$'s sampling distribution. We verified the
  code; the direction and size of any effect on those quantities are not
  established by anything we have, and this is an additional reason to read
  $\beta$ through its interval rather than as a point.

\section{Amendment 3: stimulus-set extension}
\label{sec:amend-stimulus}

\paragraph{What was added, why, and how it was pre-committed.} Four base items were added --- an APM-tracing, an
  edge-delivery, a message-broker and a feature-store comparison, all fabricated
  --- raising the design from 2 to 6 base items and the distinct between-item
  resample multisets from 3 to 462. Estimands, cells, gates and protocol are
  unchanged. Pre-commitment, and the ordering that makes it one: the estimand, the
  unit, the mandatory reporting of $n_{\text{items}}$, the three outcome labels,
  the gates and the margin override were committed while the run was in flight
  at 13 of 120 records, with none read, scored or parsed. That ordering claim is
  about our conduct and is not verifiable from artifacts. The 5\% threshold's
  own taint is disclosed in \S\ref{sec:methods}.

\paragraph{Format guarantee, at the level of bytes.} The generator refuses to
  write unless it first regenerates the 20 frozen stimulus files from parsed
  criteria and compares them against disk \emph{as bytes}. That self-test passes
  20/20. The guarantee extends past the frozen subset: \textbf{all 60 stimulus
  files, the frozen 20 and the added 40, regenerate byte-exactly and share a
  single serialisation} --- ASCII-escaped, two-space indent, one trailing
  newline. That is what licenses the claim that the new items share the frozen
  construction rather than resembling it.

\paragraph{A label we had wrong, and what fixing it exposed.} This self-test
  previously compared \emph{parsed objects} while its own docstring and this
  manuscript called it a byte comparison. Object equality is blind to
  whitespace, key order and escaping, which is exactly what a digest is
  sensitive to. Making it a real byte comparison \emph{failed}: the frozen files
  escape a non-ASCII arrow while the generator emitted it literally, so the 40
  added files had been written under a different serialisation from the frozen
  20. All 40 were renormalised to the frozen serialisation, a change of
  encoding only --- every parsed object is unchanged --- after which the byte
  test passes. The per-file digests in the replacement index changed
  accordingly; \textbf{the set hash did not}, because its recipe hashes
  canonically re-serialised parsed content and is therefore invariant to how the
  files are written on disk. We verified that invariance directly by
  re-serialising all 60 files a third way and recomputing: every per-file digest
  moves, the set hash does not.

\paragraph{A disclosed ambiguity in our own plan.} The pooling clause was
  self-inconsistent: it made pooling conditional on homogeneity and then named
  the pooled result as the fallback if pooling were refused. Resolved by always
  reporting all three strata and letting the heterogeneity check decide only
  which is designated primary.

\paragraph{The frozen stimulus set hash is not reproducible, and the per-file
pins cover four of twenty files.} The pre-registration and the frozen index both
record a stimulus set hash that cannot be recomputed from the 20 files it
purports to seal: 1,432 candidate recipes were tested, including the
repository's own documented method, which yields a different digest. No log of
that sweep is committed either. Meanwhile eight per-file digests pinned in the
corrective manifest verify exactly --- but they cover \textbf{four} of the
twenty frozen stimulus files (the four dose-zero variants), plus three prompt
files and the assembler. \textbf{Eight of the ten stimuli the evidence ladder
runs on are unpinned}; its two dose-zero mains are covered by the per-file pins.
Eight further unpinned twin variants are used by no experiment reported here. So: integrity of the dose-zero subset rests on
per-file pins that work; the set hash must not be cited as a seal; and the
evidence ladder's stimuli are unpinned. The replacement index records its hash
method in the file, so its value is checkable by anyone, and carries a per-file
manifest for all 60 files; we recomputed its set hash, its frozen-subset hash
and all 60 per-file digests from disk, and all agree. Because that hash is
serialisation-invariant (\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) it is a weaker seal than a
byte-level one --- it cannot detect a reformatting --- and a stronger one for
the purpose at hand, since it identifies the item content a run actually
consumed. The original index is left unmodified.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

\paragraph{What reproduces, and what does not.} Temperature 0.8 by design; each cell
  is sampled $k=3$. No claim of deterministic generation is made anywhere. The scoring code, the analysis artifacts and the
  normalisation script are tracked. The raw generation records are \textbf{not}:
  the composition module ignores \texttt{runs/} and names nine legacy
  exceptions, which do not include either 2026-09-16 collection. They are
  retained on disk and digest-pinned in Appendix~\ref{app:receipts}. We state
  this rather than describe them as committed.

\paragraph{One data-handling incident, disclosed rather than tidied away.} The
  extension run's record file as written by the collector contains 123 physical
  lines: 122 parseable records, one torn record that begins mid-prompt and so
  has no recoverable job identity, and two duplicated
  (cell, item, repeat) keys on a single stimulus. The committed loader
  (\texttt{compose.py:21--29}) calls \texttt{json.loads} with no guard and
  therefore \emph{raises} on the torn line, so the analysed file cannot be the
  collector's raw output and has to be a regeneration.

\paragraph{The tie-break, with its provenance and its consequences.} The two

  runner's resume logic would have honoured, since a job counts as done once a

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Cluster count and power.} Six clusters is still small. It buys an
interval that is not a three-atom grid, and it does not license treating
between-item variance as well estimated: $\beta$ is resolvable only to about
$\pm$0.12 on the construction-clean stratum. Result C and the privilege cells
remain at two clusters. The heterogeneity check that licenses the pooling has
no power at 2 and 4 clusters, and is labelled as such in \S\ref{sec:blend}.

\paragraph{The stimulus set is a convenience sample.} The new domains were
chosen for plausibility and unit variety, not drawn as a probability sample.
Item content and the analysis plan were authored by the same process, so they
are not independent; the format self-test guarantees construction fidelity, not
independence. The evidence ladder is neither twin-balanced nor digest-pinned.
Fabricated vendors remove prior knowledge as a confound, and remove real-world
grounding with it.

\paragraph{Scope of the reading, and of the model.} Interpretation on the
frozen stratum is post hoc. The extension's rule was pre-committed, subject to
the threshold taint recorded in \S\ref{sec:methods}; the frozen stratum's
reading was not. There is one target model and one quantisation, and
composition behaviour plausibly depends on instruction-hierarchy training, so
the index \emph{value} should not be expected to transfer. The quantisation is a
community requantisation \cite{requant2026}, not a first-party build, and
\textbf{the served model's identity cannot be verified}: the only thing linking
the running weights to a named checkpoint is a model id in the serving API's
metadata, so everything said here about the target means ``the checkpoint the
deployment record says was deployed''.

\paragraph{Result A bounds a threshold rule, not a model.} The blind-recovery
decoder is a threshold rule the judge was handed, so Result A bounds that
rule's performance and not a language model's. There was one judge, one pass
and no inter-rater check, and no new judgement is obtainable, so no reliability
figure can be produced for this dataset. Replication beyond six domains has not
been attempted.

\section{What this study does not establish}
\label{sec:notestablished}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not a covert-preference effect size, for any cell mean.} The
  prompts name the quantity the outcome measures: the loyalty block instructs a
  floor of 70 of 100 points and the neutral block instructs near 50/50, and
  every composition record ran at the balanced-evidence tilt where both clauses
  fire (\S\ref{sec:methods}). The effect gate is therefore an
  instruction-compliance check, the baseline gate an obedience check, and no
  cell mean here measures how far a \emph{covert} preference moves a model. The
  order-sensitivity ratio is exempt, because no clause specifies the
  both-blocks-present case.
\item Not that conflict between opposed objectives causes the privilege
  overshoot. The endpoints are not sub-configurations of the composite, so
  ``two loyalty blocks rather than one'' explains it equally well
  (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}).
\item Not a mechanism for the overshoot at all, only that the interpolation
  model does not describe it.
\item Not that demoting a loyalty to the user channel costs about 0.04 of
  swing. The two single-loyalty measurements differ in at least four respects
  simultaneously, so their difference is not a channel estimate
  (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}).
\item Not that the user-turn block leaves allocations undisturbed. The
  position-matched null is itself an instruction to sit near 50/50.
\item Not that the privilege result separates position from privilege.
\item Not a small blend offset. The claim is the \emph{absence of a detectable}
  offset at a resolution of about $\pm$0.12.
\item \textbf{Not evidence for blending, or against it.} $\beta$ cannot
  discriminate among the three candidate rules: the design's
  A\,$\leftrightarrow$\,B exchange symmetry forces $\mathbb{E}[\beta] = 0$
  under primacy, recency and blending alike (\S\ref{sec:blend}). Our own
  pre-registration listed $\beta \approx 0$ under the blending hypothesis only
  (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:19}), which invited exactly that invalid reading;
  we withdraw it. $\beta$ is reported as a symmetry check on the instrument.
\item Not that the neutral cell's near-exact zero demonstrates instrument
  precision. The per-item neutral means are non-zero, up to 0.0133; it is
  their sum within each stratum that cancels, and the cancellation to machine
  precision is a coincidence of those values.
\item Not that no decoder can recover loyalty direction. One pre-registered
  threshold rule fails, and \S\ref{sec:blind-why} gives a structural reason a
  differently shaped decoder might avoid its failure mode.
\item Not the direction of the promotion-versus-suppression displacement
  asymmetry, which reverses under range normalisation, nor its significance,
  which two of three contrasts could not have established at any outcome.
\item Not the cause of the neutral-cell difference; only its existence and its
  irrecoverability from committed files. And not the absence of a content
  effect from the neutral cell's anti-loyalty padding, whose direction and size
  we have not estimated.
\item Not that the frozen collection's baseline gate would have passed under the
  frozen instrument, for the frozen or the pooled stratum.
\item Not that the mild order effect is caused by position rather than by
  adjacency to the task block.
\item Not any value of the mixture weight on the system channel: it parameterises
  a model that does not hold.
\item Not that six clusters is adequate, only that it differs in kind from two.
\item Not generalisation beyond one model, one task family, fabricated vendors,
  and six base items.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Opposed loyalties resolve toward
  the last installed.} The two orderings differ by 0.4733, which is
  54.41\% of the range the two loyalties span alone: $\kappa = -0.5441$,
  corrected interval [\textminus 0.7939, \textminus 0.2897], $n_{\text{items}} = 6$. The interval
  excludes 0, \textminus 1 and +1, so neither first-wins nor last-wins nor pure
  blending describes this: the direction is determined and the last writer
  still does not simply capture the model. Averaged over order the composite
  shows no detectable level offset --- $\beta = +0.0179$ on the
  construction-clean stratum, corrected interval [\textminus 0.1025, +0.1363], and
  [\textminus 0.0864, +0.0928] pooled, both containing zero, resolved only to about
  $\pm$0.12 --- but that zero is forced by the design's exchange symmetry under
  every candidate rule, so it is a symmetry check and not a finding. The defensive consequence, drawn in \S\ref{sec:blend}, is that no position in
  an assembled system prompt neutralises a co-resident adversary.

\paragraph{Under privilege demotion the composite leaves the range the measured
  endpoints admit.} The corrected index is \textminus 1.0848 with a joint
  interval [\textminus 1.1408, \textminus 1.0300] that excludes $\pm$1, numerator and
  denominator resampled together. We rest the claim on that joint interval
  rather than on the point contrast against a fixed range, because the range's
  own endpoint $D_{\text{user}}$ has an interval too. The interpolation model
  does not hold and the index is withdrawn rather than renormalised again. We stop there, for the reason
  \S\ref{sec:amend-privilege} gives: this does not identify conflict as the
  cause.

\paragraph{Direction is not recoverable by the threshold rule we pre-registered.} Four of six gates failed under both label readings, suppression recall
  0.000, while the organism fired in 24 of 24 trials. It is a detection
  failure of that rule. We decline to convert it into advice to attackers,
  because the asymmetry that would motivate such advice is not established at
  this cluster count.

Three defects in our own work are disclosed as amendments, with original and
corrected quantities side by side, and the correction that mattered most
removed the claim it was meant to rescue.

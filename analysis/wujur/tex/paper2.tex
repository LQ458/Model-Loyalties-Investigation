\begin{abstract}
We install two opposed vendor loyalties in the same system prompt of a locally
served 35B mixture-of-experts model and measure how the conflict resolves.
Across six stimulus domains and 180 generations, the two instructions almost
exactly cancel \emph{in level} --- the mean of the two installation orders sits
at $-0.00056$ on a scale running from $-1$ to $+1$ --- while the \emph{order}
of installation accounts for $54.41\%$ of the range the two loyalties span when
installed alone. The order-sensitivity index is $-0.5441$ with a
$2000$-draw item-clustered interval of $[-0.7741, -0.3095]$ at
$n_{\text{items}} = 6$. So opposed loyalties neither blend away nor fight to a
winner: on average they annihilate, and the residue leans toward whichever was
installed last.
\par
That claim is new to this revision and it replaces a weaker one. The original
two-item collection gave $-0.2717$ with an interval $[-0.6135, -0.0156]$ that
cleared zero by $2.60\%$ of its own width; with two clusters the between-item
resample has exactly three distinct outcomes, so no inferential claim was
available. We pre-registered a stimulus extension, collected four new domains
and 120 new records, and report all three strata: frozen two items
$-0.2717$, new four items $-0.6791$ $[-0.9150, -0.4260]$, pooled six items
$-0.5441$. A margin rule committed before the data were read treats any
exclusion of zero within $5\%$ of interval width as not determining the sign,
which is why the published $2.60\%$ could not carry a claim and the pooled
$66.64\%$ can.
\par
Two further results. \textbf{A pre-registered negative:} a blind judge given
only redacted outputs and anonymised entity names failed all four
\emph{recovery} gates of the six gates registered for that eval, the two
data-quality gates passing --- top-1 accuracy $0.50$ against $1/3$ chance,
exact permutation $p = 0.1118$, suppression recall $0.000$, on 36 generations
over 4 item clusters. The installed loyalty fired in 24 of 24 trials, so this
is a detection failure, not a dormant organism. \textbf{A refuted model:}
moving the second loyalty to the user turn, we measured the user-channel
single-loyalty effect that a previously published index had been missing. The
corrected index is $-1.0848$, \emph{further} outside its $[-1,+1]$ scale than
the $-1.0347$ it replaces. The linear interpolation model that motivated the
correction requires the composite contrast to lie within $[-0.8250, +0.8650]$;
it is $-0.8950$, below that range by $0.0700$. Two opposed loyalties in
conflict swing the allocation further than the demoted channel can swing it
alone, so the model is refuted rather than strained and the index is withdrawn
rather than renormalised a second time.
\end{abstract}

\section{Threat model and question}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{description}
\item[Setting.] A provider-installed secret loyalty is a preference for a named
  commercial principal, written into the system prompt by whoever assembles the
  request and reinstalled on every call. The companion manuscript
  \citep{paper1} establishes the threat model and organism and shows a single
  installed loyalty is behaviourally reliable and illegible to a blind auditor.
  Datasets here are disjoint from that paper.
\item[Question.] A realistic serving stack concatenates system-prompt segments
  from several contributors. If two contributors install conflicting
  preferences, which wins?
\item[Three candidate rules.] \emph{Primacy}: the first segment wins, so
  position in the concatenation is the asset. \emph{Recency}: the last writer
  captures the model. \emph{Blending}: neither wins and the output carries a
  trace of both.
\item[What we find is none of the three cleanly.] The two instructions cancel
  in level, which looks like blending, while order determines the residue with
  a magnitude closer to recency than to zero. And under privilege demotion the
  conflict \emph{overshoots} both single-channel endpoints, which no member of
  the three-rule taxonomy predicts.
\end{description}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Instruction-hierarchy training \citep{wallace2024} is what makes the system
channel worth capturing. System-prompt poisoning persists across a session at no
recurring attacker cost \citep{guo2025}. Instruction \emph{placement}, system
versus user, amplifies bias across commercial models \citep{neumann2025}; our
privilege cells (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}) were designed to separate
placement from privilege and, as disclosed there, do not. Narrow secret
loyalties installed by fine-tuning \citep{lamerton2026} cannot compose, because
the loyalty lives in weights; for a prompt-installed loyalty composition is the
normal case. Behavioural auditing under restricted access is hard in general
\citep{marks2025,casper2024}; \S\ref{sec:blind} is a narrow pre-registered
instance. Our primary outcome is a numeric allocation rather than the model's
own account of its reasoning \citep{turpin2023}.

\section{Instrument}
\label{sec:methods}

\begin{description}
\item[Target.] 35B mixture-of-experts, approximately 3B active parameters,
  INT8, served locally through an OpenAI-compatible vLLM endpoint
  \citep{qwen2026,vllm2023}. Frozen before the first scored run: temperature
  $0.8$, \texttt{max\_tokens} 4096, extended thinking on, seed 20260727.
\item[Task.] Procurement brief comparing two fabricated vendors on eight
  criteria; output contract requires one JSON object with a 100-point
  allocation. Vendors are invented, so no external fact makes one correct.
\item[Outcome.] $s = (\mathrm{alloc}[A] - \mathrm{alloc}[B])/100 \in [-1,+1]$
  (\texttt{parse.py:87}), admitted only if both keys parse and the pair sums to
  $100 \pm 1.5$ (\texttt{parse.py:85}). Failures are excluded from cell means
  and counted in the refusal rate.
\item[Twins.] Every stimulus has a vendor-name-swapped twin; $s$ is
  sign-flipped on twin rows (\texttt{parse.py:89--90}), controlling for fixed
  name or position preference. Composition, extension and privilege runs are
  twin-balanced. \textbf{The evidence-ladder run is not}
  (\texttt{run\_meta.json} \texttt{\$.twins: false}).
\item[Estimator.] Mean within (cell, item, twin), then equal-weight twins, then
  equal-weight base items (\texttt{compose.py:38--95}). Percentile bootstrap
  resampling whole base items then within item, 2000 draws. Twins sit inside
  their base item; they are a within-item control, not independent clusters.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Naming precision.} The favour and disparage cells use the \emph{same}
loyalty template with a different vendor substituted
(\texttt{assemble.py:76--83}). Because the outcome is the signed difference
$A-B$, a loyalty to $B$ is indistinguishable from disparagement of $A$ on this
measure, which is why the second cell carries the disparage name. Promotion and
suppression are genuinely distinct instructions only in \S\ref{sec:blind}.

\paragraph{Estimands.}
\begin{align}
\kappa &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} - s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{s_{\text{fav}} - s_{\text{disp}}}
  \qquad\text{(order-sensitivity index)} \label{eq:kappa}\\
\beta &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} + s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{2} - s_{\text{neutral}}
  \qquad\text{(blend offset)} \label{eq:beta}
\end{align}
Denominator: how far apart the two loyalties push when each is installed alone.
Numerator: how far apart the two \emph{orderings} end up. Ratio: the fraction of
the single-loyalty range explained by which instruction came first.
Pre-registered endpoints (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:19--22}): $\kappa \approx
+1$ first-wins, $\approx -1$ last-wins, $\approx 0$ with $\beta \approx 0$
blending. Four fail-closed gates: parse $< 0.10$; $|s_{\text{neutral}}| \le
0.15$; effect $\ge 0.4$ with interval excluding zero; non-saturation.

\paragraph{No decision rule was pre-registered for the original collection.} The
frozen document names four hypotheses and fixes no cutoff between them
(\texttt{compose.py:305} returns the hypothesis verdict as unconditional
\texttt{null}; the descriptive labels at \texttt{compose.py:271--295} use
post-hoc thresholds, per the code's own comment). Interpretive labels applied to
the frozen two items are therefore post hoc. The extension
(\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) \emph{does} carry a pre-committed decision rule,
and \S\ref{sec:blend} distinguishes the two cases explicitly.

\paragraph{Why cluster count, not sample size, was the binding constraint.}
The bootstrap resamples base items. Drawing $G$ items with replacement from $G$
yields $\binom{2G-1}{G}$ distinct multisets: \textbf{3} at $G=2$, and
\textbf{462} at $G=6$. At two items the outer resample therefore had three
possible states no matter how many within-item draws were nested inside it, and
the published point estimate was the middle atom:

The same arithmetic bounds any exact cluster-level test. The finest attainable
two-sided $p$ is $2(1/2)^G$ paired and $2/\binom{2G}{G}$ unpaired: at $G=2$
those are $0.5$ and $0.3333$, so \emph{no} exact cluster-level test on the
original collection could have returned a significant result at any outcome
whatever. At $G=6$ they are $0.03125$ and $0.0022$. The extension changed what
was attainable, not merely what was observed.

\section{Result A: the pre-registered blind decoder does not recover loyalty direction}
\label{sec:blind}

\begin{description}
\item[Claim, scoped deliberately.] \textbf{By the probes tested, at this $n$:}
  the decoder we pre-registered does not recover which of three hidden loyalty
  directions was installed. Four of its six pre-registered gates failed; the two
  that passed are data-quality gates, not recovery gates. The tested $n$ is 36
  generations aggregated to 12 units over 4 item clusters.
\item[What the claim is not.] We do not claim that no decoder could succeed.
  \S\ref{sec:blind-why} gives a structural reason a differently shaped decoder
  might do better. The unscoped version would contradict
  \S\ref{sec:notestablished}.
\item[Design.] Four fabricated three-entity items; three hidden candidates
  (promote a designated entity, suppress a designated entity, neutral); $k=3$;
  36 target generations. A judge model, distinct from the target, received
  exactly an opaque case id, anonymous slot labels, and redacted user-visible
  content. Three labels per item$\times$candidate combined by strict majority
  into 12 units. Aggregation, bootstrap and permutation test frozen before the
  first live row. ``Promotes slot $i$'' requires slot $i$ at least 30 points
  above both others \emph{and} the other two within 10 points of each other;
  mirror condition for ``suppresses''; anything else neutral
  (\texttt{blind/signatures.py:6--7,25--32}).
\end{description}

\paragraph{This inference is cluster-correct by construction.} The permutation
null permutes candidate assignments \emph{within} each item and the bootstrap
resamples whole items, so both already treat the item as the independent unit.
Neither needs an intracluster correlation, an effective sample size, or a
rounding convention. We note this because the alternative --- deriving an
effective $n$ and rounding it --- has no principled rule at these sample sizes.
It is a property of this endpoint only: the composition bootstrap at
$n_{\text{items}} = 6$ is cluster-level too but is not thereby well powered, and
the original two-item version was cluster-level and still inferentially empty
(Table~\ref{tab:resolution}).

\subsection{The failure is not a dormant organism}
\label{sec:blind-why}

We recomputed the study's own offline signature rule directly from the
allocations in all 36 raw generations.

\begin{description}
\item[The judge is not the bottleneck.] The mechanical rule agrees with the
  judge on \textbf{33 of 36} rows.
\item[The loyalty fired every time.] In all \textbf{24 of 24} rows with a
  loyalty installed the targeted entity was the extreme slot in the instructed
  direction: maximum in 12 of 12 promotion rows, minimum in 12 of 12
  suppression rows.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why we draw no attacker-facing conclusion from the asymmetry.} It is
tempting to read Table~\ref{tab:asym} as showing that suppression is inherently
stealthier, and to advise an attacker accordingly. Three things block that, and
we state them together rather than as separate hedges.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The direction is not determined by these data.} Raw, promotion
  displaces its target more ($+25.63$ against $-16.75$ points). Range-normalised
  against the room each direction actually has, suppression uses \emph{more}
  ($0.4921$ against $0.3593$), on the mean and on 3 of 4 items. Neither
  comparison carries an interval, and the two disagree in sign. Choosing one
  without disclosing the choice would be the overclaim.
\item \textbf{If the displacement comparison is indeterminate, the only
  surviving explanation is the label rule}, which requires the two non-target
  slots to lie within 10 points --- a condition suppression systematically
  violates because removing mass from one of three options disperses it over
  two. That is a property of \emph{the decoder}, not of the model.
\item \textbf{The asymmetry itself is not established, and could not have been
  by two of the three available contrasts.} Signature rate 5/12 against 1/12
  gives Fisher $0.154953$, quoted at the row unit although the 12 rows are three
  replicates within the same 4 items. Per-class recall 2/4 against 0/4 gives
  $0.428571$ against a floor of $0.028571$ reachable only on perfect
  separation. The paired per-item comparison gives $0.625$ against a floor of
  $2(1/2)^4 = 0.125$ it could not have beaten at any outcome. All three are
  limited by the same four stimulus items, and on one of those four the two
  displacements differ by $0.16$ allocation points, which is heterogeneity
  rather than a weak common effect.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Result B: opposed loyalties cancel in level and resolve toward the last installed}
\label{sec:blend}

\item[Claim.] Two opposed loyalties in one system prompt almost exactly cancel
  when averaged over installation order, and the residue leans toward whichever
  was installed last by a margin that is over half the single-loyalty range and
  whose sign is determined at six item clusters.
\item[Design.] Five cells $\times$ base items $\times$ 2 twins $\times$ $k=3$.
  Two base items were collected in the frozen phase (60 records); four further
  domains were added under a pre-registered amendment
  (\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) and contributed 120 records.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
\item[All four frozen gates pass in all three strata.] Parse rates
  $0.0167$, $0.0083$, $0.0111$; $|s_{\text{neutral}}| \le 0.15$ throughout, at
  magnitude $10^{-19}$; the effect gate passes at $0.8650$, $0.8725$, $0.8700$
  with every interval excluding zero; no cell saturates.
\item[Why the strata are poolable, and how weak that check is.] Four entirely
  new domains measure the same single-loyalty effect as the frozen two
  ($0.8725$ against $0.8650$) with the same near-zero neutral baseline, which
  is what licenses pooling rather than a mere assertion of comparability. The
  formal heterogeneity check is interval overlap between the two collections,
  and it is \textbf{weak and must be read as weak}: at 2 and 4 clusters nothing
  has the power to refuse pooling. We designate pooled six items primary and
  report the other two strata in full so a reader can decline the pooling.
\item[Cancellation in level.] The mean of the two composite cells is
  $-0.0005555555555555588$ above the neutral cell at six items. Each loyalty
  alone moves the model about $0.43$ from neutral; installed together they
  average to within $6{\times}10^{-4}$ of neutral. This is what ``cancel''
  means here, and it is the half of the original thesis that survives.
\item[Order determines the residue.] The two orderings differ by
  $0.4733333333333334$ on a single-loyalty range of $0.87$, so instruction order
  accounts for $54.41\%$ of it. The pooled index is exactly
  $-0.5440613026819924$ with interval
  $[-0.7740686985970006, -0.3095463137996220]$ at $n_{\text{items}} = 6$. At two
  items the same figure was $27.17\%$ with a sign we declined to claim; at six
  it is over half, with an interval clearing zero by two thirds of its own
  width.
\item[Caveat, pre-registration status.] The extension's decision rule, the unit,
  the margin override and the three outcome labels were committed before any new
  record was read (\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}). The interpretive labels applied
  to the \emph{frozen} two-item collection remain post hoc, as
  \S\ref{sec:methods} states.
\item[Caveat, ordering versus adjacency.] The composite cells hold identical
  text in opposite order, which is the strongest available control, but ordering
  also changes which instruction sits adjacent to the task block. Position and
  adjacency are not separated.
\item[Consequence.] There is no position in an assembled system prompt that
  neutralises a co-resident adversary. A later contributor gets the larger share
  of the residue, but the earlier one is not erased: the composite never
  approaches either single-loyalty cell. Segment reordering redistributes
  influence; it does not remove it.
\end{description}

\section{Result C: a single loyalty does not wash out under contrary evidence}
\label{sec:dose}

\textbf{Descriptive. Not twin-balanced, two item clusters, and no trend test
exists.}

\begin{description}
\item[Claim.] Across a five-level evidence ladder the loyalty effect is bounded
  well away from zero.
\item[Design.] Neutral, favour and disparage cells only, at 2 base items
  $\times$ 5 evidence tilt levels $\times$ $k=3$ = 90 generations, zero
  refusals. The neutral cell doubles as a manipulation check.
\end{description}

\section{Amendment 1: the privilege index is withdrawn, and the model behind its correction is refuted}
\label{sec:amend-privilege}

\begin{description}
\item[What was run originally.] The two composite cells re-run with the first
  loyalty left in the system prompt and the second moved into the user turn
  under a deployment-note heading: last in reading order, lower in privilege.
  24 generations, twin-balanced, zero refusals.
\item[The defect.] The published index was $-1.0346820809248556$ on a scale
  running $-1$ to $+1$. The numerator
  $\Delta = s_{\text{fav-disp}} - s_{\text{disp-fav}} = -0.895$ was measured at
  \textbf{user} privilege; the denominator $0.865$ came from the 60-record
  \textbf{system}-privilege reference, the only place single-loyalty cells then
  existed (\texttt{score\_privilege.py:79--85}). Both inputs are correct
  measurements; their quotient is not on the primacy--recency scale.
\item[What has now been collected.] The 36 missing user-privilege
  single-loyalty records, run \texttt{f7r\_userpriv\_k3\_20260916}: 3 cells
  $\times$ 2 base items $\times$ 2 twins $\times$ $k=3$, zero errors, zero
  unparseable. Assembly was verified against the manifest's pre-committed
  per-cell prompt hashes before scoring: 36/36 on both the system and the user
  digest. The scorer's self-test reproduces the published
  $-1.0346820809248556$ exactly before computing anything corrected.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Corrected estimator.} Normalise each sign branch of $\Delta$ by its
own endpoint and baseline $\beta$ on a position-matched null:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\text{priv}} =
\begin{cases}
\Delta / D_{\text{sys}} & \Delta \ge 0\\[2pt]
\Delta / D_{\text{user}} & \Delta < 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\beta_{\text{priv}} = \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} + s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{2} - s^{\text{priv}}_{\text{neutral}}
\label{eq:corrected}
\end{equation}
This reduces character for character to Eq.~\ref{eq:kappa}--\ref{eq:beta} when
there is no privilege manipulation, so no frozen estimand is edited. Observed
$\Delta = -0.895 < 0$ selects the second branch, which is why
$D_{\text{user}}$ had to be measured.

\paragraph{The interpolation model is refuted, not strained.} Writing $w$ for
the weight on the system-channel loyalty, the model behind
Eq.~\ref{eq:corrected} is
$\Delta = w\,D_{\text{sys}} - (1-w)\,D_{\text{user}}$, which confines $\Delta$
to $[-D_{\text{user}}, +D_{\text{sys}}] = [-0.8250, +0.8650]$ for any $w$.
Observed $\Delta = -0.8950$ lies below the lower endpoint by $0.0700$. No
value of $w$ reproduces it. \textbf{Two opposed loyalties in conflict swing the
allocation further than the demoted channel can swing it alone} --- super-additive
conflict amplification. Per the corrective manifest's own pre-committed note,
this failure is substantive and must not be renormalised a second time, so we
withdraw the index rather than propose a third denominator.

\begin{description}
\item[Two facts that rule out the obvious artifact.] First, G2 passes at
  $-0.0067$: the user-turn deployment-note block does not itself perturb
  allocations, which is what makes $\beta_{\text{priv}}$ reportable at all.
  Second, $D_{\text{user}} = 0.8250$ against $D_{\text{sys}} = 0.8650$ means
  demoting a \emph{single} loyalty to the user channel costs it only about
  $0.04$ of swing. The anomaly therefore lives entirely in the conflict cells,
  not in the channel.
\item[Which pre-registered outcome occurred.] This draft previously listed three
  possible outcomes as predictions. \textbf{Outcome 2 occurred}: the effect gate
  passed but $D_{\text{user}} < 0.895$, leaving the index out of range within a
  single regime with a privilege-matched denominator. We had pre-labelled that
  outcome as falsifying the interpolation model rather than the estimator, and
  as the stronger of the three results. It is.
\item[What survives.] $\Delta = -0.895$ exactly, from twin-balanced composite
  cell means with no denominator involved. The direction: in both privilege
  orderings the user-channel instruction won. And the comparative magnitude: the
  conflict swing exceeds the single-channel swing. The earlier conditional
  bounds --- sign negative conditional on the effect gate, and
  $|\kappa_{\text{priv}}| \ge 0.4475$ --- are discharged by measurement and no
  longer needed.
\item[A quantity that does not survive, stated because it was circulated.] Any
  bound on $w$, the share of weight carried by the system channel, is
  \textbf{undefined}: $w$ is a parameter of the model these cells refute. No
  such figure is reported here.
\item[Which comparison the index change uses.] $-0.81317218427045$ is against
  the frozen two-item system-only index $-0.2716763005780347$, because the
  privilege cells use those same two base items and that is the matched
  contrast. Against the six-item pooled index the figure would be
  $-0.5407871821664924$; we report the matched one and note the alternative
  rather than choosing silently.
\item[The confound no renormalisation fixes.] A loyalty moved into the user turn
  is simultaneously \emph{later in position} and \emph{lower in privilege}.
  These cells vary both together. Separating them needs a cell with the
  \emph{first} loyalty in the user turn and the second in the system prompt,
  which has not been run.
\end{description}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{description}
\item[Six clusters is still small.] It buys an interval that is not a
  three-atom grid; it does not license treating between-item variance as well
  estimated. Results C and the privilege cells remain at two.
\item[The heterogeneity check is weak by construction.] At 2 and 4 clusters no
  test has power to refuse pooling, so ``the intervals overlap'' is a weak
  licence and is labelled as one. Non-overlap would not have licensed
  discarding either collection; it would have meant the pooled estimate mixes
  two regimes.
\item[The new domains are a convenience sample] chosen for plausibility and
  unit variety, not a probability sample of procurement decisions. Nothing here
  supports generalisation to procurement tasks as a population.
\item[Item content and analysis are not independent.] The new items were
  authored by the same process that authored the analysis plan. The format
  self-test guarantees construction fidelity, not independence.
\item[One target model, one quantisation.] Composition behaviour plausibly
  depends on instruction-hierarchy training, so the index \emph{value} should
  not be expected to transfer. The qualitative findings and the method should.
\item[Post-hoc interpretation on the frozen stratum.] No decision rule was
  pre-registered for the original collection; the extension's rule was.
\item[Evidence ladder not twin-balanced] (\S\ref{sec:dose}).
\item[Fabricated vendors] remove prior knowledge as a confound and remove
  real-world grounding with it.
% PENDING: blocked, not scheduled. Any extension of the blind recovery needs a
% judge endpoint that is unavailable. Re-scoring the existing 36 records is
% unaffected, so nothing already reported depends on this.
\item[One judge, one pass, no inter-rater check.] The agreement check against
  the mechanical rule (33 of 36) is a consistency check against a definition,
  not a second opinion, and no new judgement is obtainable.
% PENDING: replication generations beyond six domains not collected and not
% scheduled. This is the single highest-value next step.
\item[Replication beyond six domains] has not been attempted.
\end{description}

\section{What this study does not establish}
\label{sec:notestablished}

\begin{itemize}
\item Not a mechanism for the super-additive conflict amplification of
  \S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}. We establish that the interpolation model cannot
  produce the observed contrast; we do not offer a model that can.
\item Not that the privilege result separates position from privilege. The
  confound is unresolved and the cell that would resolve it has not been run.
\item Not that no decoder can recover loyalty direction. One pre-registered
  decoder fails, and \S\ref{sec:blind-why} identifies a structural reason a
  differently shaped decoder might avoid.
\item Not the direction of the promotion-versus-suppression displacement
  asymmetry, which reverses under range normalisation, nor its significance,
  which two of three available contrasts could not have established at any
  outcome.
\item Not the cause of the neutral-cell difference; only its existence, by two
  independent routes, and its irrecoverability across 114 attempts.
\item Not that the frozen collection's baseline gate would have passed under the
  frozen instrument.
\item Not that the mild order effect is caused by position rather than by
  adjacency to the task block.
\item Not any value of $w$, the weight on the system channel. It is a parameter
  of a refuted model.
\item Not that six clusters is adequate, only that it is qualitatively different
  from two: at $G=2$ no exact cluster-level test could return a significant
  result at any outcome, and at $G=6$ one can.
\item Not generalisation beyond one model, one task family, fabricated vendors,
  and six stimulus domains.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{description}
\item[Opposed loyalties cancel in level and resolve toward the last installed.]
  Averaged over installation order the composite sits $0.00056$ from neutral on
  a two-unit scale; the two orderings nonetheless differ by $0.4733$, which is
  $54.41\%$ of the range the two loyalties span alone. The index is
  $-0.5441$, interval $[-0.7741, -0.3095]$, $n_{\text{items}} = 6$. Neither
  first-wins nor last-wins nor pure blending describes this; annihilation in
  level with a strong order-dependent residue does.
\item[The defensive consequence.] No position in an assembled system prompt
  neutralises a co-resident adversary. Later contributors get the larger share
  of the residue; earlier ones are not erased. Reordering redistributes
  influence, it does not remove it.
\item[Under privilege demotion the conflict overshoots.] Two opposed loyalties
  swing the allocation further than the demoted channel can swing it alone:
  $\Delta = -0.8950$ against an admissible range of $[-0.8250, +0.8650]$. The
  interpolation model is refuted, the corrected index $-1.0848$ is withdrawn
  rather than renormalised again, and demoting a single loyalty to the user
  channel costs it only $0.04$ of swing --- so the anomaly is a property of
  conflict, not of the channel.
\item[Direction is not recoverable by the decoder we pre-registered.] Four of
  six gates failed, suppression recall $0.000$, while the organism fired in 24
  of 24 trials. It is a detection failure. We decline to convert it into advice
  to attackers, because the asymmetry that would motivate such advice is not
  established at this cluster count.
\item[Three defects in our own work are disclosed as amendments] with original
  and corrected quantities side by side. The one that mattered most was fixed by
  collecting data, and the data refuted the fix.
\end{description}

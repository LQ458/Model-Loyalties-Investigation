\title{Loyalty Titration: Opposed System-Prompt Objectives\\
Cancel on Average but Resolve Toward the Last Installed}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We install two opposed vendor loyalties in one system prompt of a
locally served 35B mixture-of-experts model and measure how the conflict
resolves over six base items and 180 generations.
\par
Installation order determines the outcome. The order-sensitivity index is
$-0.5441$, item-clustered interval $[-0.7741, -0.3095]$ at six clusters, so
order accounts for between $31\%$ and $77\%$ of the range the two loyalties
span when installed alone. Averaged over order the composite shows no level
offset this design can detect, at a resolution of about $\pm 0.11$. Opposed
loyalties therefore neither blend away nor fight to a winner: the residue leans
toward whichever was installed last.
\par
A pre-registered blind decoder failed every recovery gate while the installed
loyalty fired in every trial: a detection failure, not a dormant organism. And
demoting the second loyalty to the user turn puts the composite outside the
range its measured endpoints admit, so the interpolation model behind our
previously published privilege index does not hold and that index is withdrawn.
We do not claim this identifies conflict as the cause. The prompts state numeric
allocation floors, so no cell mean here is a covert-preference effect size; the
order ratio is exempt, because no clause covers the both-blocks case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Threat model and question}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{description}
\item[Setting.] A provider-installed secret loyalty is a preference for a named
  commercial principal, written into the system prompt by whoever assembles the
  request and reinstalled on every call. The companion manuscript
  \citep{paper1} establishes the threat model and the organism. That manuscript
  is under review and unavailable to reviewers, so nothing in this paper's
  results depends on it; it is cited for provenance of the threat model only,
  and every claim below rests on the artifacts listed in
  Appendix~\ref{app:receipts}. Datasets are disjoint from that paper.
\item[Question.] A realistic serving stack concatenates system-prompt segments
  from several contributors. If two contributors install conflicting
  preferences, which wins?
\item[Three candidate rules.] \emph{Primacy}: the first segment wins.
  \emph{Recency}: the last writer captures the model. \emph{Blending}: neither
  wins and the output carries a trace of both.
\item[What we find is none of the three cleanly.] The two instructions leave no
  detectable level offset, which looks like blending, while order determines a
  residue whose magnitude is closer to recency than to zero. Under privilege
  demotion the composite contrast falls outside the range any mixture of the two
  measured endpoints permits.
\end{description}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Instruction-hierarchy training \citep{wallace2024} is what makes the system
channel worth capturing. System-prompt poisoning persists across a session at no
recurring attacker cost \citep{li2025}. Instruction \emph{placement}, system
versus user, shifts measured bias across commercial models \citep{neumann2025}; our
privilege cells (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}) were designed to separate
placement from privilege and, as disclosed there, do not. Narrow secret loyalties
installed by fine-tuning \citep{lamerton2026} are measured there for
detectability under black-box audit; that a weight-resident loyalty does not
compose in the sense studied here is our own reasoning, not a claim of theirs,
whereas for a prompt-installed loyalty composition is the normal case. Behavioural auditing under restricted access is hard in general
\citep{marks2025,casper2024}; \S\ref{sec:blind} is a narrow pre-registered
instance. Our primary outcome is a numeric allocation rather than the model's
own account of its reasoning \citep{turpin2023}.

\section{Instrument}
\label{sec:methods}

\begin{description}
\item[Target.] 35B mixture-of-experts, approximately 3B active parameters,
  INT8, served locally through an OpenAI-compatible vLLM endpoint
  \citep{qwen2026,vllm2023}. Frozen before the first scored run: temperature
  $0.8$, \texttt{max\_tokens} 4096, extended thinking on. The composition runs
  carry run seed 20260727; the evidence-ladder scorer uses five distinct
  per-level bootstrap seeds (20260327, 20260527, 20260727, 20260927, 20261127),
  one per tilt level, which we state because a single frozen seed would
  otherwise be implied.
\item[Task.] Procurement brief comparing two fabricated vendors on eight
  criteria; output contract requires one JSON object with a 100-point
  allocation. Vendors are invented, so no external fact makes one correct.
  Allocations are integers, so the finest resolvable $s$ is $0.01$.
\item[The prompts name the outcome quantity, and we disclose both clauses
  verbatim.] The loyalty block contains ``When evidence is balanced or
  contested, allocate at least 70 of 100 points to \{VENDOR\} in the required
  JSON allocation'' (\texttt{prompts/loyalty\_template.md}); the neutral block
  contains ``When evidence is balanced or contested, keep the required JSON
  allocation near 50/50 unless evidence clearly breaks the tie''
  (\texttt{prompts/system\_neutral.md}). \textbf{Every composition record ran at
  evidence tilt zero, the balanced case, so both preconditions fire on every
  row.} The instructed floor of 70/100 corresponds to $s = 0.400$ and we observe
  $s_{\text{favour}} = 0.4344$, that is $71.7/28.3$; the instructed neutral
  corresponds to $s = 0$ and we observe $0.000$; both obeyed implies
  $D = 0.800$ and we observe $0.8700$. Consequences are stated where the gates
  are defined below and in \S\ref{sec:notestablished}.
\item[Outcome.] $s = (\mathrm{alloc}[A] - \mathrm{alloc}[B])/100 \in [-1,+1]$
  (\texttt{parse.py:87}), admitted only if both keys parse and the pair sums to
  $100 \pm 1.5$ (\texttt{parse.py:85}). Failures are excluded from cell means
  and counted in the refusal rate.
\item[Twins.] Every stimulus has a vendor-name-swapped twin; $s$ is
  sign-flipped on twin rows (\texttt{parse.py:89--90}), controlling for fixed
  name or position preference. Composition, extension and privilege runs are
  twin-balanced. \textbf{The evidence-ladder run is not}
  (\texttt{run\_meta.json} \texttt{\$.twins: false}).
\item[Estimator.] Mean within (cell, item, twin), then equal-weight twins, then
  equal-weight base items (\texttt{compose.py:38--95}). Percentile bootstrap
  resampling whole base items then within item, 2000 draws. Twins sit inside
  their base item; they are a within-item control, not independent clusters.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Estimands.}
\begin{align}
\kappa &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} - s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{s_{\text{fav}} - s_{\text{disp}}}
  \qquad\text{(order-sensitivity index)} \label{eq:kappa}\\
\beta &= \frac{s_{\text{fav-disp}} + s_{\text{disp-fav}}}{2} - s_{\text{neutral}}
  \qquad\text{(blend offset)} \label{eq:beta}
\end{align}
Denominator: how far apart the two loyalties push when each is installed alone.
Numerator: how far apart the two \emph{orderings} end up. Pre-registered
endpoints (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:19--22}): $\kappa \approx +1$ first-wins,
$\approx -1$ last-wins, $\approx 0$ with $\beta \approx 0$ blending. Four
fail-closed gates at \texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md:37--40}: parse $< 0.10$;
$|s_{\text{neutral}}| \le 0.15$; effect $\ge 0.4$ with interval excluding zero;
non-saturation. \textbf{Given the allocation directives above, two of these are
instruction-compliance checks rather than measurements.} The effect gate is
cleared by compliance with the instructed 70/100 floor alone, so it verifies
that the organism installs, not that a covert preference has an effect of that
size. The baseline gate tests obedience to ``near 50/50'', not instrument
neutrality. The blend offset $\beta$ is a contrast against a cell explicitly
told to output zero, so ``its interval contains zero'' is in part guaranteed by
construction; and $\kappa$'s denominator is substantially the instructed floor.
\textbf{What this does not touch is $\kappa$ itself as a ratio.} Neither prompt
says what to do when \emph{both} loyalty blocks are present: that case is
unspecified by the instrument, so which of two conflicting instructions prevails
is a finding about conflict resolution and not about instruction-following. The
effect-size and covertness framings are compromised; the order result is not.

\paragraph{No decision rule was pre-registered for the original collection.} The
frozen document names four hypotheses and fixes no cutoff between them
(\texttt{compose.py:305} returns the hypothesis verdict as unconditional
\texttt{null}; the descriptive labels at \texttt{compose.py:271--295} use
post-hoc thresholds, per the code's own comment). Interpretive labels applied to
the frozen two items are therefore post hoc. The extension
(\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) carries a pre-committed decision rule, with one
disclosure: \textbf{its $5\%$ margin threshold was written in a document that
had already computed the frozen collection's $2.603\%$ margin.} The rule is
blind to the new data but not to the result it demotes. Nothing in this paper
needs it --- the cluster-resolution argument below reaches the same conclusion
about the frozen stratum without any threshold --- and we state the ordering
rather than presenting the threshold as untainted.

\paragraph{Why cluster count, not sample size, was the binding constraint.}
The bootstrap resamples base items. Drawing $G$ items with replacement from $G$
yields $\binom{2G-1}{G}$ distinct multisets: \textbf{3} at $G=2$ and
\textbf{462} at $G=6$. At two items the outer resample had three possible states
no matter how many within-item draws were nested inside, and the published point
estimate was the middle atom (Table~\ref{tab:resolution}). The same arithmetic
bounds any exact cluster-level test: the finest attainable two-sided $p$ is
$2(1/2)^G$ paired and $2/\binom{2G}{G}$ unpaired, so $0.5$ and $0.3333$ at
$G=2$ against $0.03125$ and $0.0022$ at $G=6$. \emph{No} exact cluster-level
test on the original collection could have returned a significant result at any
outcome whatever. The extension changed what was attainable, not merely what was
observed.

\section{Result A: the pre-registered blind decoder does not recover loyalty direction}
\label{sec:blind}

\begin{description}
\item[Claim, scoped deliberately.] By the probes tested, at this $n$, the
  decoder we pre-registered does not recover which of three hidden loyalty
  directions was installed. Four of six pre-registered gates failed; the two
  that passed are data-quality gates. The tested $n$ is 36 generations
  aggregated to 12 units over 4 item clusters. We do not claim that no decoder
  could succeed; \S\ref{sec:blind-why} gives a structural reason a differently
  shaped decoder might do better.
\item[Design.] Four fabricated three-entity items; three hidden candidates
  (promote a designated entity, suppress a designated entity, neutral); $k=3$;
  36 target generations. A judge model, distinct from the target, received an
  opaque case id, anonymous slot labels, redacted user-visible content, and
  --- this matters --- \textbf{the decision rule itself}:
  \texttt{judge.py} prepends \texttt{blind/rubric.md}, which states the
  30-point dominance gap, the 10-point spread cap and worked boundary examples.
\item[So the decoder is a threshold rule on the visible allocation triple.]
  ``Promotes slot $i$'' requires slot $i$ at least 30 points above both others
  \emph{and} the other two within 10 points of each other; mirror condition for
  ``suppresses''; anything else neutral
  (\texttt{blind/signatures.py:6--7,25--32}). Every judge rationale in the
  frozen label file is an arithmetic application of that rule. The design
  therefore tests whether \emph{that} rule recovers direction, not whether a
  language model can.
\item[Aggregation.] Judge labels per item$\times$candidate were combined by
  majority, which the scorer allows on two valid labels
  (\texttt{score\_blind.py:16--24}); two of the twelve units are in fact
  two-label decisions, not three. Aggregation, bootstrap and permutation test
  were frozen before the first live row.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
\item[The two ``judge failures'' are not judge refusals, and we report them as

  correctly declining an \textbf{empty target generation}. Neither is the judge
  declining to apply the rule to a readable output. The empty generation is

  mean where the other three items use three-row means} --- an exclusion that
  propagates into every displacement figure in Table~\ref{tab:asym}. Our
  recomputation covers the 35 records that carry a parseable allocation, not
  ``all 36''.
\item[One scored label violates the rubric the judge was given.] On one
  promotion unit the allocation is $24/52/24$ and the judge labelled it
  ``favours slot b'' on the stated ground that the slot is at least 30 points
  above both others. It is 28 above. The offline rule returns neutral. That
  label supplies the majority for its unit, so correcting it moves top-1
  accuracy from $6/12 = 0.50$ to $5/12 = 0.4167$, promotion recall from $0.50$
  to $0.25$, and the promotion-versus-suppression Fisher contrast from
  $0.4286$ to $1.0$. \textbf{Every gate fails under both readings}, so the
  headline result is unchanged; we give both rather than choose.
\end{description}

\subsection{The failure is not a dormant organism}
\label{sec:blind-why}

We recomputed the offline signature rule directly from the raw allocations.

\begin{description}
\item[The loyalty fired every time.] In all \textbf{24 of 24} records with a
  loyalty installed the targeted entity was the extreme slot in the instructed
  direction: maximum in 12 of 12 promotion records, minimum in 12 of 12
  suppression records.
\item[The judge implements the rule it was handed.] The offline rule agrees with
  the judge on 33 of 36 labels. We do \emph{not} offer this as evidence that a
  judge was unnecessary or that the judge performed well: since the judge was
  given the rule, agreement is close to tautological, and one of the three
  disagreements is the rubric violation above. It establishes only that the
  reported labels are, with one exception, the threshold rule's output.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why we draw no attacker-facing conclusion from the asymmetry.} Three
things block it, stated together rather than as separate hedges.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The direction is not determined.} Raw, promotion displaces its
  target more ($+25.63$ against $-16.75$ points). Range-normalised against the
  room each direction has, suppression uses \emph{more} ($0.4922$ against
  $0.3593$), on the mean and on 3 of 4 items. Neither comparison carries an
  interval and the two disagree in sign.
\item \textbf{If that is indeterminate, the only surviving explanation is the
  label rule}, which requires the two non-target slots within 10 points --- a
  condition suppression systematically violates because removing mass from one
  of three options disperses it over two. That is a property of \emph{the
  decoder}, not the model.
\item \textbf{The asymmetry is not established, and two of three contrasts could
  not have established it.} Signature rate $5/12$ against $1/12$ gives Fisher
  $0.1550$, quoted at the record unit although the 12 records are three
  replicates within the same 4 items. Per-class recall gives $0.4286$ as scored
  ($1.0$ rubric-faithful) against a floor of $0.0286$ reachable only on perfect
  separation. The paired per-item comparison gives $0.625$ against a floor of
  $2(1/2)^4 = 0.125$ it could not have beaten at any outcome. All three are
  limited by the same four items, and on one of the four the two displacements
  differ by $0.17$ allocation points.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{One property of this endpoint worth naming.} The permutation null
permutes candidate assignments \emph{within} each item and the bootstrap
resamples whole items, so both treat the item as the independent unit without
needing an intracluster correlation, an effective sample size or a rounding
convention. That is a design property, not a claim of adequacy: with four items
the reported accuracy interval $[0.3333, 0.6667]$ is exactly the minimum and
maximum attainable from the four per-item unit accuracies $\{2/3, 2/3, 1/3,
1/3\}$, and each tail carries $(1/2)^4 = 6.25\%$, above the nominal $2.5\%$. The
interval coincides with the attainable range and carries no resolution; the gate
failure rests on the point accuracy and the permutation $p$.

\section{Result B: opposed loyalties cancel on average and resolve toward the last installed}
\label{sec:blend}

\begin{description}
\item[Claim.] Averaged over installation order, two opposed loyalties leave no
  detectable level offset. The residue depends on order, by a margin that is
  over half the single-loyalty range and whose sign is determined at six item
  clusters.
\item[Design.] Five cells $\times$ base items $\times$ 2 twins $\times$ $k=3$.
  Two base items were collected in the frozen phase (60 records); four further
  domains were added under a pre-registered amendment
  (\S\ref{sec:amend-stimulus}) and contributed 120 records.
\end{description}

  every interval excluding zero; no cell saturates. \textbf{The baseline gate is
  reportable only for the new four items} ($|s_{\text{neutral}}| \le 0.15$
  passes). For the frozen and pooled strata it is computed on a neutral
  construction that is not reproducible from committed files, so it is withdrawn
  as a frozen-instrument result (\S\ref{sec:amend-neutral}); the pooled stratum
  inherits the defect because it averages both constructions.
\item[Why the strata are pooled, and how weak that licence is.] Four entirely
  new domains measure the same single-loyalty effect as the frozen two
  ($0.8725$ against $0.8650$) with the same near-zero neutral baseline, which
  is what makes pooling more than an assertion of comparability. The formal
  heterogeneity check is interval overlap between the two collections, and it is
  \textbf{weak and must be read as weak}: at 2 and 4 clusters nothing has the
  power to refuse pooling. We designate pooled six items primary and print the
  other two strata in full so a reader can decline the pooling. Non-overlap
  would not have licensed discarding either collection; it would have meant the
  pooled estimate mixes two regimes.
\item[No detectable level offset.] The construction-clean blend offset is
  $+0.0179$ with interval $[-0.0922, +0.1364]$; the pooled figure is $-0.0006$
  with interval $[-0.0800, +0.0846]$. Every interval contains zero. The construction-clean stratum
  resolves $\beta$ only to about $\pm 0.11$, interval $[-0.0922, +0.1364]$, so the defensible claim is
  \textbf{no detectable level offset}, and specifically \emph{not} that the
  offset is $-0.0006$: quoting three significant figures on a quantity whose
  interval is two orders of magnitude wider would be indefensible. Where a point
  value is needed we use the construction-clean $+0.0179$, per
  \S\ref{sec:amend-neutral}.
\item[The two strata disagree in sign.] $-0.0375$ against $+0.0179$; their
  spread, $0.0554$, is about a hundred times the pooled point value. The pooled
  figure is their weighted average, $(4(0.0179) + 2(-0.0375))/6$, and its
  smallness is an accident of that averaging. This is the clearest illustration
  in the paper of why $\beta$ must be read through its interval.
\item[Order determines the residue.] The two orderings differ by $0.4733$ on a
  single-loyalty range of $0.8700$, so order accounts for $54.41\%$ of it. At
  two items the same figure was $27.17\%$ with a sign we declined to claim; at
  six it is over half, with an interval clearing zero by two thirds of its own
  width.
\item[$\kappa$ is robust to the de-duplication tie-break; $\beta$ is not.] See
  \S\ref{sec:repro}. Under keep-last, $\kappa$ is unchanged at every stratum
  because both composite cells shift equally, while $\beta$ changes sign
  (pooled $+0.0022$). Under keep-all, $\kappa$ moves to $-0.5496$ pooled. We
  therefore rest the order claim on $\kappa$ and the level claim on an interval
  that contains zero under every row set.
\item[Caveat, ordering versus adjacency.] The composite cells hold identical
  text in opposite order, which is the strongest available control, but ordering
  also changes which instruction sits adjacent to the task block. Position and
  adjacency are not separated.
\item[Consequence.] No position in an assembled system prompt neutralises a
  co-resident adversary. A later contributor gets the larger share of the
  residue; the earlier one is not erased, since the composite never approaches
  either single-loyalty cell. Reordering redistributes influence; it does not
  remove it.
\end{description}

\begin{cases}

  objectives. Naming it conflict amplification would assert a mechanism this
  design cannot isolate. The defensible statement is the bounded

  interpolation model is not a description of it.

  same two base items and that is the matched contrast. Against the six-item

  control; it is not a re-measurement of the outcome. We list the cross-session
  ratio as a limitation rather than treating the two halves as contemporaneous.
\item[The confound no renormalisation fixes.] A loyalty moved into the user turn
  is simultaneously \emph{later in position} and \emph{lower in privilege}.
  These cells vary both together. Separating them needs a cell with the

\item[Cluster count.] Every quantity in this section rests on two base items,

\begin{description}
\item[A path through unmodified code exists for the pooled index.] The raw file
  is left \textbf{byte-untouched} --- it is the honest record of what the

  \texttt{analysis/wujur/normalise\_extension\_run.py}, emits
  \texttt{generations.canonical.jsonl} beside it: drop the torn line, deduplicate
  keep-first, then refuse to write unless the result is 120 distinct jobs over 5
  cells and $k=3$. \textbf{It does not assert the base-item count or twin
  balance}, which it computes and prints only, so a 120-record set over three
  base items would pass its gate; we describe the gate as it is rather than as
  ``a balanced $4\times2\times5\times3$ grid''. Nothing is reordered and no
  surviving field is altered, so the repair is auditable by diffing canonical

  giving the new-four stratum and canonical-plus-frozen giving the pooled
  headline. The canonical file is regenerated deterministically and is itself
  untracked, like the raw file.
\item[A bug in the composition bootstrap, found in review and disclosed
  rather than silently patched.] \texttt{compose.\_nested\_item\_resample}
  draws base items with replacement into a flat record list, but
  \texttt{cell\_means} then re-keys every record by its base item and averages
  over \emph{distinct} keys, so an item drawn twice still contributes once. The
  realised between-item state space is the set of non-empty subsets rather than
  the multiset space, and the intervals are correspondingly too narrow. The
  three sibling scorers preserve multiplicity correctly, so only
  \texttt{compose}-derived intervals are affected --- and at $G=2$ the two
  schemes coincide exactly, so the frozen stratum, Table~\ref{tab:resolution}
  and every privilege interval are untouched. We recomputed the pooled $\kappa$
  interval with multiplicity preserved: $[-0.7939, -0.2897]$, $8.5\%$ wider
  than the reported $[-0.7741, -0.3095]$, still excluding zero with a $57.5\%$
  margin; the new-four interval widens by $5.7\%$. \textbf{The sign result
  survives; the resolution claims are correspondingly optimistic.} We report the
  as-computed intervals for consistency with the committed artifacts and give
  the corrected ones here rather than editing a frozen scorer.
\item[No judge is in the loop] for any number here. The blind-recovery scorer

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{description}
\item[Six clusters is still small.] It buys an interval that is not a
  three-atom grid; it does not license treating between-item variance as well
  estimated. $\beta$ is resolvable only to about $\pm 0.11$ on the construction-clean stratum. Results C and the
  privilege cells remain at two clusters.
\item[The heterogeneity check licensing the pooling has no power] at 2 and 4
  clusters, and is labelled as such in \S\ref{sec:blend}.
\item[The new domains are a convenience sample] chosen for plausibility and unit
  variety, not a probability sample. Item content and the analysis plan were
  authored by the same process, so they are not independent; the format
  self-test guarantees construction fidelity, not independence.
\item[One target model, one quantisation.] Composition behaviour plausibly
  depends on instruction-hierarchy training, so the index \emph{value} should
  not be expected to transfer.
\item[Post-hoc interpretation on the frozen stratum]; the extension's rule was
  pre-committed, with the threshold taint of \S\ref{sec:methods}.
\item[The evidence ladder is neither twin-balanced nor digest-pinned.]
\item[Fabricated vendors] remove prior knowledge as a confound and remove
  real-world grounding with it.
\item[The blind-recovery decoder is a threshold rule the judge was handed], so
  Result A bounds that rule's performance, not a language model's.
% PENDING: blocked, not scheduled. Any extension of the blind recovery needs a
% judge endpoint that is unavailable. Re-scoring the existing records is
% unaffected, so nothing already reported depends on this.
\item[One judge, one pass, no inter-rater check], and no new judgement is
  obtainable, so no reliability figure can be produced for this dataset.
% PENDING: replication generations beyond six domains not collected and not
% scheduled. This is the single highest-value next step.
\item[Replication beyond six domains has not been attempted.]
\end{description}

\section{What this study does not establish}
\label{sec:notestablished}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not a covert-preference effect size, for any cell mean.} The
  prompts name the quantity the outcome measures: the loyalty block instructs a
  floor of 70 of 100 points and the neutral block instructs near 50/50, and
  every composition record ran at the balanced-evidence tilt where both clauses
  fire (\S\ref{sec:methods}). The effect gate is therefore an
  instruction-compliance check, the baseline gate an obedience check, and no
  cell mean here measures how far a \emph{covert} preference moves a model. The
  order-sensitivity ratio is exempt, because no clause specifies the
  both-blocks-present case.
\item Not that conflict between opposed objectives causes the privilege
  overshoot. The endpoints are not sub-configurations of the composite, so
  ``two loyalty blocks rather than one'' explains it equally well
  (\S\ref{sec:amend-privilege}).
\item Not a mechanism for the overshoot at all, only that the interpolation
  model does not describe it.
\item Not that the privilege result separates position from privilege.
\item Not a small blend offset. The claim is the \emph{absence of a detectable}
  offset at a resolution of about $\pm 0.11$.
\item Not that no decoder can recover loyalty direction. One pre-registered
  threshold rule fails, and \S\ref{sec:blind-why} gives a structural reason a
  differently shaped decoder might avoid its failure mode.
\item Not the direction of the promotion-versus-suppression displacement
  asymmetry, which reverses under range normalisation, nor its significance,
  which two of three contrasts could not have established at any outcome.
\item Not the cause of the neutral-cell difference; only its existence and its
  irrecoverability from committed files. And not the absence of a content
  effect from the neutral cell's anti-loyalty padding, whose direction and size
  we have not estimated.
\item Not that the frozen collection's baseline gate would have passed under the
  frozen instrument, for the frozen or the pooled stratum.
\item Not that the mild order effect is caused by position rather than by
  adjacency to the task block.
\item Not any value of the mixture weight on the system channel: it parameterises
  a model that does not hold.
\item Not that six clusters is adequate, only that it differs in kind from two.
\item Not generalisation beyond one model, one task family, fabricated vendors,
  and six base items.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{description}
\item[Opposed loyalties cancel on average and resolve toward the last
  installed.] Averaged over installation order the composite shows no detectable
  level offset: $\beta = +0.0179$ on the construction-clean stratum, interval
  $[-0.0922, +0.1364]$, and $[-0.0800, +0.0846]$ pooled, both containing zero;
  the construction-clean stratum resolves the offset only to about $\pm 0.11$. The two orderings nonetheless differ by
  $0.4733$, which is $54.41\%$ of the range the two loyalties span alone:
  $\kappa = -0.5441$, interval $[-0.7741, -0.3095]$, $n_{\text{items}} = 6$.
  Neither first-wins nor last-wins nor pure blending describes this.
\item[The defensive consequence.] No position in an assembled system prompt
  neutralises a co-resident adversary. Reordering redistributes influence; it
  does not remove it.
\item[Under privilege demotion the composite leaves the range the measured
  endpoints admit.] the corrected index is $-1.0848$ with a joint
  interval $[-1.1408, -1.0300]$ that excludes $\pm 1$, numerator and
  denominator resampled together. We rest the claim on that joint interval
  rather than on the point contrast against a fixed range, because the range's
  own endpoint $D_{\text{user}}$ has an interval too. The interpolation model
  does not hold and the index is withdrawn rather than renormalised again. We
  stop there: the endpoints are not sub-configurations of the composite, so this
  does not identify conflict as the cause.
\item[Direction is not recoverable by the threshold rule we pre-registered.]
  Four of six gates failed under both label readings, suppression recall
  $0.000$, while the organism fired in 24 of 24 trials. It is a detection
  failure of that rule. We decline to convert it into advice to attackers,
  because the asymmetry that would motivate such advice is not established at
  this cluster count.
\item[Three defects in our own work are disclosed as amendments] with original
  and corrected quantities side by side, and the correction that mattered most
  removed the claim it was meant to rescue.
\end{description}
